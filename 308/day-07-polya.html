How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.10

Elements of finite order

Exercise 4.5.10. Find all elements of finite order in each of the following groups. Here the \(\ast\) indicates the set with zero removed.
(a) \(\mathbb Z\)
(b) \(\mathbb Q^\ast\)
(c) \(\mathbb R^\ast\)

Goal: Apply the definition of order to a group you know well, then to two groups where the answer isn’t visible at a glance. It uses the definition of the order of an element from Section 4.1, and Euclid’s Lemma (Lemma 2.2.5) for part (b).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.12

Cyclic groups with few generators

Exercise 4.5.12. Find a cyclic group with exactly one generator. Can you find cyclic groups with exactly two generators? Four generators? How about \(n\) generators?

Goal: Count generators with Corollary 4.1.14, and see which numbers of generators are possible. It uses Corollary 4.1.14 (the generators of \(\mathbb Z_n\) are the \(r\) with \(1 \le r < n\) and \(\gcd(r, n) = 1\)) and the gcd facts from Chapter 2.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.13

Which groups of units are cyclic

Exercise 4.5.13. For \(n \le 20\), which groups \(U(n)\) are cyclic? Make a conjecture as to what is true in general. Can you prove your conjecture?

Goal: Build the table of groups of units, decide which are cyclic by looking for an element of the right order, and state a conjecture you can test on the table. It uses the group of units \(U(n)\) from Section 3.1, the definition of a cyclic group, and the order formula in Theorem 4.1.13.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.24

Generators of Z_pq

Exercise 4.5.24. Let \(p\) and \(q\) be distinct primes. How many generators does \(\mathbb Z_{pq}\) have?

Goal: Count the generators of \(\mathbb Z_{pq}\) by subtracting the elements that fail, and state the count as a formula in \(p\) and \(q\). It uses Corollary 4.1.14 and two facts from Chapter 2: Euclid’s Lemma, and that a prime dividing both \(r\) and \(pq\) must be \(p\) or \(q\).
