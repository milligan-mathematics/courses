How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

Something new: the axiom is a given. The Nested Interval Property is assumed, not proved, so no step asks you to prove it. Some steps ask for numbers instead: a midpoint, a decimal truncation, the first index that works. The page checks each one, so you can test a truncation or a midpoint before you commit to it.

The walk-throughs end in a full proof, so the proof you bring to class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 9.1.2

Two sequences that never cross

Problem 9.1.2. Let \((x_n), (y_n)\) be sequences as in the Nested Interval Property. Show that for all \(n, m \in \mathbb{N}\), \(x_n \le y_m\).

Goal: Chain \(x_n\) up to \(y_m\) through the larger of the two indices, using only the order properties of the NIP, and cover every pair \((n, m)\). Nothing here needs a limit or the number \(c\). The two sequences are already in order, and the first three properties of the NIP say so.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 9.1.3

A hole at the square root of 2

Problem 9.1.3. (a) Find two sequences of rational numbers \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) which satisfy properties 1–4 of the NIP and such that there is no rational number \(c\) satisfying the conclusion of the NIP.
(b) Find two sequences of rational numbers \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) which satisfy properties 1–4 of the NIP and such that there is a rational number \(c\) satisfying the conclusion of the NIP.

Goal: Build rational sequences from the decimal digits of \(\sqrt{2}\), show that no rational \(c\) can be trapped by them, and then find a rational example for part (b). This is the hole the reading describes: the rational numbers fail the NIP, and the real numbers fill the gap. Keep that in mind when Problem 9.1.7 builds a square root from the NIP.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 9.1.5

Squeezed onto the limit

Problem 9.1.5. Prove Theorem 9.1.4. Suppose that \((x_n)\) and \((y_n)\) satisfy all the assumptions of the Nested Interval Property. If \(c\) is the unique number such that \(x_n \le c \le y_n\) for all \(n\), then \(\lim_{n\to\infty} x_n = c\) and \(\lim_{n\to\infty} y_n = c\).

Goal: Turn the sandwich \(x_n \le c \le y_n\) into two limits, using property 4 to choose \(N\) and the definition of a limit to finish. The day page calls this the step that makes the NIP usable: the NIP hands you a \(c\), and this theorem says both endpoint sequences really close in on it. The next problem uses it at once.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 9.1.7

Bisect, and never write the root

Problem 9.1.7. Turn the outline of the bisection proof into a formal proof of Theorem 9.1.6.
Theorem 9.1.6. Suppose \(a \in \mathbb{R}\), \(a \ge 0\). There exists a real number \(c \ge 0\) such that \(c^2 = a\).

Goal: Build \(c\) from \(a\): bisect to get the NIP sequences, then use property 5 to show \(c^2 = a\). The proof may not use \(\sqrt{a}\), because its existence is what is being proved. The reading says the IVT and EVT proofs use this same strategy: bisect, let the NIP give \(c\), and let a fifth property say which \(c\) you get.
