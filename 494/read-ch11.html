Chapter 11 · A

Who gets to the beach?

A. Chapter 11, “Freedom.” Su opens with a picture book about the beach, read to eight children in Los Angeles, only one of whom had ever been to the beach. The beach becomes his metaphor for five freedoms at the heart of doing mathematics, and for the ways a space can say “Open to all” and still feel restricted.

Goal: Name Su’s five freedoms and the virtues they build, then decide what you make of his claim about welcome. Freedom of welcome leads straight into Chapter 12, “Community,” where Su asks what makes people feel they belong in mathematics.

Chapter 11 · Puzzle

Unknown Polynomial

Puzzle. I’m thinking of a polynomial whose coefficients are all nonnegative whole numbers, like \(x^3 + 2x + 7\). You don’t know its degree. You may ask only questions of the form “What is the value of the polynomial at \(k\)?” for a specific number \(k\). What is the fewest number of questions you need to determine my polynomial? (Su, Chapter 11.)

Goal: Find a strategy that always works with as few questions as possible, and see why it works. The key idea is place value: writing a number in base \(b\) is exactly evaluating a polynomial with digit coefficients at \(x = b\).
