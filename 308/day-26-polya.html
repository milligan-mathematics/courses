How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 12.4.16

Which wallpaper group fits the chevron pattern

Exercise 12.4.16. Determine which of the \(17\) wallpaper groups preserves the symmetry of the pattern in the figure below.
(The figure is the chevron wallpaper in the exercise in the online book, Exercise 12.4.16: rows of chevrons, the top row pointing downward, the second row pointing upward, and so on.)

Goal: Find the lattice of the pattern and its point group, decide which flips are true mirrors and which need a slide, and name the group from Table 12.2.11. It uses lattices, point groups and glide reflections from Section 12.2, the shape of the translation group (Theorem 12.2.8), and the list of the \(17\) wallpaper groups (Theorem 12.2.12, Table 12.2.11).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 12.4.17

Which wallpaper group fits the pattern of hexagons

Exercise 12.4.17. Determine which of the \(17\) wallpaper groups preserves the symmetry of the pattern in the figure below.
(The figure is the lattice of hexagons in the exercise in the online book, Exercise 12.4.17: each hexagon is divided into three rhombuses.)

Goal: Find the lattice, the largest turn and the flips of the pattern, and name the group from Table 12.2.11. A hexagon’s outline is only part of what has to land on itself. It uses lattices, point groups and the list of the \(17\) wallpaper groups from Section 12.2 (Theorems 12.2.9 and 12.2.12, Table 12.2.11), and the dihedral groups \(D_n\) from Chapter 3.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 12.4.19

A wallpaper pattern for each of the 17 groups

Exercise 12.4.19. For each of the \(17\) wallpaper groups, draw a wallpaper pattern having that group as a symmetry group.

Goal: Learn a recipe that produces a pattern for any one of the \(17\) groups, and the checks that tell you the pattern has that group and not a bigger one. You do the drawing yourself; the walk-through is about how to plan it. It uses lattices, point groups, glide reflections and the list of the \(17\) wallpaper groups (Theorems 12.2.9 and 12.2.12, Table 12.2.11) from Section 12.2, and the idea of a motif and its images under a group of motions.
