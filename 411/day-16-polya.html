How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

Something new: you type formulas. Some steps ask for a number, or for a formula in \(n\) or \(k\). Type pi for \(\pi\), sqrt(2) for \(\sqrt2\), and ^ for powers. The page checks what you type at several values, so a formula written another way is accepted.

The walk-throughs end in full proofs, so the proof you bring to class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 5.1.7

Two halves of a tent

Problem 5.1.7. Let \(n\) be a positive integer. Show that if \(f(x)=\frac{1}{2}-\left|x-\frac{1}{2}\right|\), then \(\displaystyle\int_{x=0}^1 f(x)\sin\left(n\pi x\right)\,dx = \frac{2}{\left(n\pi\right)^2}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right)\), and show that the Fourier sine series of \(f\) is \[f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{4}{\left(n\pi\right)^2}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right)\sin\left(n\pi x\right)=\frac{4}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{\left(-1\right)^k}{\left(2k+1\right)^2}\sin\left(\left(2k+1\right)\pi x\right).\]

Goal: Compute the Fourier sine coefficients of a tent function, and see which of them survive. It uses the Day 15 formula for the sine coefficients, \(A_n = 2\int_0^1 f(x)\sin(n\pi x)\,dx\). It leads to 5.1.8, where the series is differentiated twice.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 5.1.8

Term by term at one point

Problem 5.1.8. Show that when \(x = \frac14\), \[4\sum_{k=0}^\infty(-1)^{k+1}\sin\left((2k+1)\pi x\right)=4\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}+\cdots\right).\]

Goal: Evaluate a series term by term at a point, and find the pattern the terms repeat. It is the second term-by-term derivative of the series in 5.1.7. It leads to 5.1.12, where a different cosine series is differentiated in the same way.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 5.1.9

Product to sum, then two cases

Problem 5.1.9. Let \(n\) and \(m\) be positive integers. Show that \[\int_{x=0}^1\cos\left(n\pi x\right)\cos\left(m\pi x\right)\,dx=\begin{cases}0 & \text{if } n\neq m\\ \frac{1}{2} & \text{if } n=m.\end{cases}\]

Goal: Turn a product of two cosines into a sum, and integrate it in two cases. It is the cosine version of the orthogonality result 5.1.5 from Day 15. It leads to 5.1.10, where the same integrals pick out one coefficient of a cosine series.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 5.1.10

Multiply by the basis, integrate

Problem 5.1.10. Use the result of Problem 5.1.9 to show that if \(f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty B_n\cos\left(n\pi x\right)\) on \([0,1]\), then \[B_m=2\int_{x=0}^1 f(x)\cos\left(m\pi x\right)\,dx.\]

Goal: Find a formula for one coefficient of a cosine series by multiplying and integrating, and say what that step assumes. It is the cosine version of the coefficient formula from Day 15, and it gives the coefficients used in 5.1.11.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 5.1.11

A line as a cosine series

Problem 5.1.11. Apply the result of Problem 5.1.10 to show that the Fourier cosine series of \(f(x)=x-\frac{1}{2}\) on \([0,1]\) is \[\frac{-4}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{1}{\left(2k+1\right)^2}\cos\left((2k+1)\pi x\right).\] Let \(C(x,N)=\frac{-4}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=0}^N\frac{1}{\left(2k+1\right)^2}\cos\left((2k+1)\pi x\right)\) and plot \(C(x,N)\) for \(N=1,2,5,50\) and \(x\in[0,1]\). How does this compare to the function \(f(x)=x-\frac{1}{2}\) on \([0,1]\)? What if you plot it for \(x\in[0,2]\)?

Goal: Compute the cosine coefficients of a line, and predict what the plots of its series should show on \([0,1]\) and on \([0,2]\). It applies 5.1.10 to a line, and its series is the one that 5.1.12 differentiates.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 5.1.12

Differentiate twice, term by term

Problem 5.1.12. Differentiate the series \[\frac{-4}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{1}{\left(2k+1\right)^2}\cos\left((2k+1)\pi x\right)\] term by term and plot various partial sums for that series on \([0,1]\). How does this compare to the derivative of \(f(x)=x-\frac{1}{2}\) on that interval? (b) Differentiate the series you obtained in part (a) and plot various partial sums of that on \([0,1]\). How does this compare to the second derivative of \(f(x)=x-\frac{1}{2}\) on that interval?

Goal: Differentiate a cosine series term by term, twice, and predict what its partial sums do on \([0,1]\) compared with \(f^{\prime}\) and \(f^{\prime\prime}\). It differentiates the series from 5.1.11 term by term, twice, using the move the Day 16 page tests. When that move is allowed is a question for Chapter 10.
