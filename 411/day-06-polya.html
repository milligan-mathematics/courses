Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.1.15

Check the cycloid against the equation

Problem 3.1.15. Show that the equations \(x=\frac{\phi-\sin \phi}{4gc^2}\), \(y=\frac{1-\cos \phi}{4gc^2}\) satisfy the Brachistochrone equation \(\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\sqrt{2gy\left[(\mathrm{d}x)^2+(\mathrm{d}y)^2\right]}}=c\). Bernoulli recognized this solution to be an inverted cycloid, the curve traced by a fixed point on a circle as the circle rolls along a horizontal surface.

Goal: Check that a curve given by parametric formulas satisfies a differential equation at every point, by writing every differential as a multiple of \(\mathrm{d}\phi\). It finishes the reading’s story: Bernoulli solved the equation, and this problem checks that the cycloid really satisfies it. Writing \(\mathrm{d}x\) and \(\mathrm{d}y\) as multiples of \(\mathrm{d}\phi\) is the move that turns \(\mathrm{d}s\) into an expression in \(\mathrm{d}\phi\).
