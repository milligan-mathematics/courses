Coordinate Boxes

§14.5 — fill a solid with cylindrical or spherical coordinate boxes, see why a box’s volume carries the factor r or ρ² sin φ, and watch the sum close in on the integral.

Pick a solid. It is cut into m × n × k coordinate boxes: in cylindrical coordinates a box has r, θ, and z each in a small interval, and in spherical coordinates the same for ρ, φ, and θ. A box counts if its center is in the solid, and each blue dot is the center of a counted box. The sum adds f at each center times the box’s volume, and the exact integral is shown for comparison. Use the three inspect sliders to pick one box (shown in orange) and compare its true volume with the product of its three widths. Drag inside the scene to orbit it.

The sample point is the center of each box. Boxes along a curved edge of the solid are not counted exactly, so the sum only closes in on the integral as the boxes get smaller. The green outline is the coordinate box that contains the whole solid.

Things to try

  1. Pick the paraboloid bowl (cylindrical) with f = 1. Predict which boxes have the biggest true volume: those near the axis or those far from it, and by what factor. Then inspect ring 1 and the outermost ring (any wedge, any layer) and compare the true volume with r* Δr Δθ Δz.
  2. Pick the ice-cream cone (spherical), f = 1. The coordinate box is exactly the solid, so every box counts. Predict the answer you get with the factor left out (think: what is the product of the three ranges?). Then tick the box and check it against the exact volume π(2 − √3)/3 ≈ 0.2806.
  3. Pick the spherical shell sector with f = x² + y². The section page gets 31π/12 ≈ 8.116. Raise m, n, k and watch the sum settle there. Which of the three sliders matters most?
  4. Pick any solid and f = x. Before you read the exact value, decide from the shape whether it should be zero. For which solids is it not zero, and why?
  5. Pick the shifted cylinder (the page’s mass example, f = x) and look at the outline from above with the arrow keys. Why do the θ-values run only from −π/2 to π/2? The exact value is 64/15 ≈ 4.267.
