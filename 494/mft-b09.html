Paper first, then check in. Give yourself 15 minutes for the five problems on paper, no calculator, the way the real test runs. Then open each card. If you already have an answer, press Check my answer from paper: one try. Right, and you can skip to Look back for a faster route or a variation. Not right, or not sure, and the walk-through takes it one step at a time: Understand, Plan, Carry out, Look back.

Every wrong choice has a story. The test writers build the wrong answers out of the slips people actually make. When you pick one here, the page tells you which slip leads there, so you can spot it next time.

Walk-through · Set B9, Problem 1

When does the drug peak?

1. After a dose of medication, the concentration of the drug in a patient’s bloodstream is \(C(t)=20\,t\,e^{-t/2}\) milligrams per liter, \(t\) hours after the dose. At what time is the concentration greatest?
(A) \(0.5\) hours
(B) \(2\) hours
(C) \(4\) hours
(D) \(10\) hours
(E) \(20\) hours

Goal: Find the time where the concentration stops rising and starts falling. Curves like \(t\,e^{-kt}\) (a rise, then an exponential decay) model drug levels, surges and damped responses. The MFT likes them because the product and chain rules both have to go right.

Walk-through · Set B9, Problem 2

Counting generators of a cyclic group

2. How many elements of the cyclic group \(\mathbb{Z}_{20}\) generate the whole group?
(A) \(8\)
(B) \(10\)
(C) \(12\)
(D) \(19\)
(E) \(20\)

Goal: Decide which elements generate \(\mathbb{Z}_{20}\), then count them without listing all twenty. Generators of \(\mathbb{Z}_n\), units mod \(n\), and Euler’s \(\varphi\) are the same count seen three ways. The MFT asks for it in group, ring and number-theory dress.

Walk-through · Set B9, Problem 3

A race between n! and n^n

3. What is the radius of convergence of the power series \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{n^n}x^n\)?
(A) \(0\)
(B) \(\frac{1}{e}\)
(C) \(1\)
(D) \(e\)
(E) \(\infty\)

Goal: Find the radius by comparing each coefficient with the next one. Radius-of-convergence questions show up on every MFT. Ratios of factorials and powers simplify well, and the limit \(\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n \to e\) is the one to have ready.

Walk-through · Set B9, Problem 4

Reading a phase portrait from a matrix

4. For the system \(\mathbf{x}’=A\mathbf{x}\) with \(A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2\\-2&-1\end{pmatrix}\), how is the equilibrium point at the origin best described?
(A) A saddle point
(B) A center
(C) An unstable spiral
(D) A stable node
(E) A stable spiral

Goal: Find the eigenvalues of \(A\) and read the picture from their real and imaginary parts. Saddle, node, spiral, center: the MFT’s differential equations questions often come down to this table. Trace and determinant get you there without solving anything.

Walk-through · Set B9, Problem 5

A formula for a recurrence

5. The sequence \((a_n)\) satisfies \(a_0=1\), \(a_1=4\), and \(a_n=5a_{n-1}-6a_{n-2}\) for \(n\ge 2\). Which of the following is a formula for \(a_n\)?
(A) \(a_n=1+3n\)
(B) \(a_n=4^n\)
(C) \(a_n=2\cdot3^n-2^n\)
(D) \(a_n=3\cdot2^n-2\cdot3^n\)
(E) \(a_n=2^n+3^n-1\)

Goal: Find the closed form from the characteristic equation, and check it against terms you compute by hand. Linear recurrences with constant coefficients are the discrete cousins of \(y'' - 5y' + 6y = 0\): same characteristic equation, \(r^n\) in place of \(e^{rt}\). The MFT asks for them in discrete math and in counting problems.
