How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

Typing answers. Some steps ask for a number or a formula. Type a fraction with a slash (7/3), a square root with sqrt (sqrt(5)), and a power with ^ (2^n). Your answer is checked as a value, so equal expressions written differently both count.

The computer parts. Problems 3.2.12, 3.2.13 and 3.2.14(b) say to use a computer algebra system. These walk-throughs don’t draw plots. They give you the values the system would print, and they ask what those values do and don’t support, which is the part the computer can’t do for you.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.2.11

Multiply the series by itself

Problem 3.2.11. Consider the series representation \(\left(1+x\right)^{\frac12} = 1 + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac12 - j\right)}{n!}x^n = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac12 - j\right)}{n!}x^n\). Multiply this series by itself and compute the coefficients for \(x^0, x^1, x^2, x^3, x^4\) in the resulting series.

Goal: Find the first five coefficients of the square of the series, and say what they show about \(\left(\sqrt{1+x}\right)^2\). It uses only what the reading has: the series itself, and multiplying series out term by term. The same bookkeeping drives 3.2.13 and 3.2.14.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.2.12

Where the partial sums can be trusted

Problem 3.2.12. Let \(S(x,M)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^M\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac12-j\right)}{n!}x^n\). Use a computer algebra system to plot \(S(x,M)\) for \(M=5,\,10,\,15,\,95,\,100\) and compare these to the graph for \(\sqrt{1+x}\). What seems to be happening? For what values of \(x\) does the series appear to converge to \(\sqrt{1+x}\)?

Goal: Decide, from values a computer algebra system would print and from a short calculation, where the partial sums appear to converge to \(\sqrt{1+x}\), and why a picture cannot settle the endpoints by itself. It uses the coefficients from 3.2.11 and the ratio of consecutive terms. The ratio test is a calculus result, not part of the reading. The question of what a picture can prove comes back with Taylor’s theorem in Chapter 4.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.2.13

Integrate the series for pi/4

Problem 3.2.13. Use the series \(\displaystyle(1+x)^{\frac12} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac12-j\right)}{n!}x^n\) to obtain the series \[\frac{\pi}{4} = \int_0^1\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx = \sum_{n=0}^\infty\left(\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac12-j\right)}{n!}\right)\left(\frac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}\right) = 1-\frac16-\frac1{40}-\frac1{112}-\frac5{1152}-\cdots\] Use a computer algebra system to sum the first 100 terms of this series and compare the answer to \(\frac{\pi}{4}\).

Goal: Substitute \(-x^2\) into the series, integrate term by term, and check the first terms. Then decide what a 100-term sum can and cannot tell you. It is the series of 3.2.11 with \(x\) replaced by \(-x^2\), and it uses \(\int_0^1 x^{2n}\,dx\) from calculus. The term-by-term integration is the step the reading takes formally, and the Look back returns to it.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.2.14

A second series for pi

Problem 3.2.14. a. Show that \(\displaystyle\int_0^{1/2}\sqrt{x-x^2}\,dx = \sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac12-j\right)}{\sqrt2\,n!\,(2n+3)\,2^n}\) and use this to show that \(\pi = 16\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac12-j\right)}{\sqrt2\,n!\,(2n+3)\,2^n}\right)\). b. We now have two series for calculating \(\pi\): the one from part (a) and the one derived earlier, \(\pi = 4\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}\). We will explore which one converges to \(\pi\) faster. Define \(S_1(N) = 16\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^N\frac{(-1)^n\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac12-j\right)}{\sqrt2\,n!\,(2n+3)\,2^n}\) and \(S_2(N) = 4\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^N\frac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}\). Use a computer algebra system to compute \(S_1(N)\) and \(S_2(N)\) for \(N = 5, 10, 15, 20\). Which one appears to converge to \(\pi\) faster?

Goal: Turn the area under \(\sqrt{x-x^2}\) into a series with the binomial series, use a circle to get \(\pi\), and then compare the two series for \(\pi\) by the sizes of their terms. It uses the term-by-term method of 3.2.13 on a new integrand, and a circle to read off an area. The comparison in part (b) is about how fast terms shrink, which is what the ratio test measures in calculus.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.2.15

Two routes to one series

Problem 3.2.15. Let \(k\) be a positive integer. Find the power series, centered at zero, for \(f(x) = \left(1-x\right)^{-k}\) by (1) differentiating the geometric series \(k-1\) times, and (2) applying the binomial series. Compare these two results.

Goal: Get the series for \((1-x)^{-k}\) two ways, by differentiation and by the binomial series, and check that the two give the same coefficients. It finishes the Day 9 work on the binomial series. The binomial series is used here with \(\alpha = -k\), a case the reading claims but has not proved. Your comparison proves it for \(\alpha = -k\), given termwise differentiation of the geometric series.
