Chapter 3 · A

A heap of stones or a house

A. Chapter 3, “Meaning.” The hired car carrying Su’s family is stuck on a bump on a dirt road in rural China, miles from anywhere, and a Martin Gardner puzzle about a truck stuck under an overpass comes to mind. From there he argues that grasping the meaning of something is “always asking about its relationship to other things,” and that “meaning is a fundamental human desire.”

Goal: Follow Su’s case that mathematical ideas get their meaning from their connections, then decide how far you agree. Exploration (Chapter 2) sends out probes; meaning is what you build from what they find. The virtues here, story building and abstraction, come back in the chapters on beauty and truth.

Chapter 3 · Puzzle

Red-Black Card Trick

Puzzle. Hand a spectator a deck of 20 cards, 10 red and 10 black, to shuffle. Without looking at the faces, deal it face down into two piles of 10 each and announce: “The number of red cards in the first pile is the number of black cards in the second pile.” She turns them over, and you’re right. Why does it always work? (Su, Chapter 3.)

Goal: Explain why the trick always works, then see what it has in common with Water and Wine. This is a conservation argument: a fixed total forces two unknown counts to be equal. You’ll meet the same move in combinatorics whenever two quantities are each “what’s left over” from the same whole.

Chapter 3 · Puzzle

Water and Wine

Puzzle. Two glasses of the same size are each half full, one with wine and one with water. Move a spoonful of wine into the water glass and stir. Then, however well it has mixed, move a spoonful of the liquid in the water glass back into the wine glass. Is there more wine in the water glass, or more water in the wine glass? (Su, Chapter 3.)

Goal: Answer the question in one concrete case, then find a reason that needs no numbers at all. The no-numbers reason is a conservation argument, the same one behind the Red-Black Card Trick. Watching what stays fixed is one of the most useful habits in mathematics and physics.
