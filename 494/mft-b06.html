Paper first, then check in. Give yourself 15 minutes for the five problems on paper, no calculator, the way the real test runs. Then open each card. If you already have an answer, press Check my answer from paper: one try. Right, and you can skip to Look back for a faster route or a variation. Not right, or not sure, and the walk-through takes it one step at a time: Understand, Plan, Carry out, Look back.

Every wrong choice has a story. The test writers build the wrong answers out of the slips people actually make. When you pick one here, the page tells you which slip leads there, so you can spot it next time.

Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back. Bring the one card that bugged you most to our check-in.

Walk-through · Set B6, Problem 1

A power times a logarithm

1. \(\displaystyle\int_1^e x\,\ln x\,dx=\)
(A) \(\frac{e^2+1}{4}\)
(B) \(\frac{e^2-1}{4}\)
(C) \(\frac{e^2}{2}\)
(D) \(\frac{e^2-1}{2}\)
(E) \(\frac{e^2}{4}\)

Goal: Integrate by parts, then keep track of both limits. Integration by parts with a logarithm or an inverse trig function as \(u\) is a staple of the MFT’s calculus section, and so is the slip of losing the lower limit.

Walk-through · Set B6, Problem 2

The hottest point on a plate

2. A thin metal plate occupies the disk \(x^2+y^2\le 1\). The temperature at the point \((x,y)\) is \(T(x,y)=x^2+2y^2-x\). What is the highest temperature on the plate?
(A) \(-\frac{1}{4}\)
(B) \(1\)
(C) \(2\)
(D) \(\frac{9}{4}\)
(E) \(3\)

Goal: Find the largest value of \(T\) on the disk by checking the inside and the edge, then comparing. Absolute extrema on a closed region are a standard MFT multivariable item: the interior critical points are only half the story, and the boundary usually holds the answer.

Walk-through · Set B6, Problem 3

Which quotient is a field?

3. Which of the following rings is a field?
(A) \(\mathbb{Z}_{15}\)
(B) \(\mathbb{Z}_2[x]/(x^2+1)\)
(C) \(\mathbb{Z}_5[x]/(x^2+1)\)
(D) \(\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^2-2x+1)\)
(E) \(\mathbb{Z}_3[x]/(x^2+1)\)

Goal: Decide each ring by asking whether its polynomial has a root in its coefficient field. “\(F[x]/(p(x))\) is a field exactly when \(p\) is irreducible” is the abstract algebra fact the MFT tests most often, along with “\(\mathbb{Z}_n\) is a field exactly when \(n\) is prime.”

Walk-through · Set B6, Problem 4

A contour around one bad point

4. Let \(C\) be the circle \(|z|=2\), traversed once counterclockwise. \(\displaystyle\oint_C\frac{e^z}{z-1}\,dz=\)
(A) \(0\)
(B) \(2\pi i\)
(C) \(2\pi i e\)
(D) \(2\pi i e^2\)
(E) \(\pi i e\)

Goal: Spot the one point where the integrand blows up, check it’s inside the circle, and apply Cauchy’s integral formula. Complex variables is a small slice of the MFT, and Cauchy’s integral formula (or the residue theorem) is most of that slice.

Walk-through · Set B6, Problem 5

Products that aren’t negative

5. On the set of integers \(\mathbb{Z}\), define a relation \(R\) by \(a\,R\,b\) if and only if \(ab\ge 0\). Which of the following properties does \(R\) have?
  I. Reflexive
  II. Symmetric
  III. Transitive
(A) I only
(B) I and II only
(C) I and III only
(D) II and III only
(E) I, II, and III

Goal: Decide each property by proving it or breaking it with small integers. Reflexive, symmetric, transitive questions show up on nearly every MFT. The counterexample usually hides at a special element, here \(0\).
