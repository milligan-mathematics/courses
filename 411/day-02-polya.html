How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

Names. The reading says “understand the problem,” “formulate a plan,” “execute the plan,” and “reflect on what you’ve done.” Here those are Understand, Plan, Carry out, and Look back.

The four problems build on each other: a fact about primes (1.3.3), then a product of many factors (1.3.4), then a fact about binomial coefficients (1.3.5), and then Fermat’s Little Theorem itself (1.3.7). Each walk-through builds a full proof during Carry out, and Look back asks what else the proof gives you. The proof you bring to class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 1.3.3

A prime that divides a product

Problem 1.3.3. Let \(p\) be a prime number and \(a, b\) positive integers such that \(p \mid (a \cdot b)\). Show that \(p \mid a\) or \(p \mid b\).

Goal: Find the case split that makes the claim easier, then use the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (Theorem 1.3.2) on the case that is left. It is the first link in the chain toward Fermat’s Little Theorem. Problem 1.3.4 extends it from two factors to \(n\) factors.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 1.3.4

From two factors to many

Problem 1.3.4. Let \(p\) be a prime number and let \(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n\) be positive integers such that \(p \mid (a_1 \cdot a_2 \cdots a_n)\). Show that \(p \mid a_k\) for some \(k \in \{1, 2, 3, \ldots, n\}\).

Goal: Find the induction that turns the two-factor case into the \(n\)-factor case, and see which problem supplies the two-factor step. It builds on Problem 1.3.3. Problem 1.3.5 uses it on three factors, \(\binom{p}{k}\), \(k!\), and \((p-k)!\).

Pólya walk-through · Problem 1.3.5

A prime divides the binomial coefficient

Problem 1.3.5. Let \(p\) be a prime number and let \(k\) be an integer with \(1 \le k \le p - 1\). Prove that \(p \mid \binom{p}{k}\), where \(\binom{p}{k}\) is the binomial coefficient \(\frac{p!}{k!\,(p-k)!}\).

Goal: Clear the denominators so that \(p\) divides a product of three factors. Then use Problem 1.3.4, and show that \(p\) cannot divide either factorial. It is the divisibility fact that Problem 1.3.7 needs: the binomial expansion of \((n+1)^p\) has these coefficients in its middle terms.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 1.3.7

Fermat’s Little Theorem, by induction

Problem 1.3.7. Prove Fermat’s Little Theorem (Theorem 1.3.6): let \(p\) be any prime number. Then \(p \mid (n^p - n)\) for all positive integers \(n\).

Goal: Induct on \(n\), with \(p\) fixed. The step is the work: expand \((n+1)^p\), subtract \(n + 1\), and show that everything except \(n^p - n\) is a multiple of \(p\). It is the end of the chain: 1.3.3 gives the two-factor step, 1.3.4 the product, and 1.3.5 the binomial coefficients. The Look back step finds the converse, which fails.
