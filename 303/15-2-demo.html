Work Along a Curve

§15.2 — bend a path through a vector field, watch the component of the field along the path, and see the line integral as the area under that graph.

The blue curve is a path from A to B; drag its four handles (or type their positions). The orange arrow at the tracer is the part of the field that points along the path, (F·T)T, where T is the unit tangent. The graph below plots F·T against arc length s. Since ∫C F·dr = ∫ (F·T) ds, the work is the signed area under that graph: orange above the axis counts positive, gray below counts negative. The scalar integral ∫C ρ ds is shown for comparison.

The curve is a cubic Bézier curve. Every integral is computed numerically along the curve (Gauss–Legendre on 8 pieces), so the readouts are accurate to the digits shown. Tick “Reverse the direction” to walk from B to A along the same curve.

Things to try

  1. Pick the swirl ⟨−y, x⟩ and the “Quarter circle of radius 2” shape. The field is tangent to the circle and has length 2 there, so predict the shape of the F·T graph, the arc length, and the work. (Compare the unit circle on the section page, where the field has length 1 and the whole circle gives 2π.) Check it, then tick “Reverse the direction” and predict what changes in the work, in the arc length, and in the mass.
  2. Keep the swirl and pick “Straight line through the origin”. Predict F·T along a path that heads straight at the center, and the work. Then drag one handle off the line a little.
  3. Pick the constant push ⟨1, 1/2⟩. Predict whether bending the path changes the work. Try three different shapes with the same A and B (drag only the two inner handles).
  4. Pick the field ⟨0, x⟩ (a vertical push that grows with x) and the “Arch” shape. Predict the sign of the work on the left half and on the right half of the arch, then read the graph. Does the total come out positive, negative, or zero?
  5. Put A and B at the same point with the swirl, so the path is a loop (type them into the handle boxes). Predict the sign of the work for a loop that goes counterclockwise, then drag the inner handles to flip the loop and check. Compare the arc length and the work.
