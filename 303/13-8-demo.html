When the Gradients Line Up

§13.8 — slide a point along a constraint and watch the level curve of f, the two gradients, and the multiplier λ meet at the extreme values.

Pick a problem, then slide the point P along the green constraint curve. The orange arrow is the gradient of f and the blue arrow is the gradient of g. The dashed green curve is the level curve of f through P. When the two arrows line up, that level curve is tangent to the constraint, and the readout gives the multiplier λ. The lower plot shows f as P moves around the curve.

Things to try

  • Predict first. Before you move the point, look at the contour map of the circle problem. Where on the green circle do you expect f to be largest? Drag the point there and see whether you were right.
  • Check λ against the algebra. Move the point to the largest value of f on the circle and read λ from the readout. Then turn on Show solutions. The algebra says λ = 5/2 there. Does the readout agree?
  • Count before you look. On the ellipse problem, predict how many places on the curve the two gradients line up. Then turn on Show solutions. Which one is the absolute minimum, and what does the plot show about the other one?
  • Connect to the page. The closest-point example on the page ends with λ = −1/2. Find a point in this demo where λ is negative, and say which way ∇f and ∇g point compared with each other.
  • Cross, don't touch. Put the point where the dashed level curve crosses the green curve, so the angle is far from 0° and 180°. Explain in one sentence why you can move along the curve to increase f from there.
