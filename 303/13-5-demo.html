Which Way Is Uphill?

§13.5 — drag a point on a contour map, aim a direction arrow, and watch the gradient, the level curve, and the directional derivative respond together.

Drag the blue point P anywhere on the contour map, and drag the tip of the blue arrow u to turn the direction around P. The orange arrow is the gradient ∇f(P), and the green curve is the level curve through P. The graph beside the map shows the directional derivative Duf(P) as u turns through a full circle. Predict first, then check.

Drag P (the blue dot) or the tip of u (the blue arrow). Click anywhere to move P. With the map focused, arrow keys move P and [ and ] turn u.

Things to try

Predict first, then check with the demo.

  1. Find uphill by eye. Untick “Show ∇f(P) and Duf(P)”. Look only at the contour map, and predict which way is steepest uphill at P. Then tick the box and compare. (Hint: the steepest direction crosses the contour lines at right angles.)
  2. Walk along the contour. Turn u until it lies along the green level curve through P. Predict Duf(P) before you read it, then check the readout and the graph. This is the “no change” case from the sin(x + y²) worked example on the §13.5 page.
  3. Predict the biggest rate. Read the size |∇f(P)|. Before you turn u, predict the largest value Duf(P) can take at this point, and the angle where it happens. Then turn u to test your prediction.
  4. Predict the steepest drop. Point u straight downhill, opposite the orange arrow. Predict the sign and the size of Duf(P) first. Then find the lowest point on the graph and compare.
