How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

Typing formulas. Some steps ask you to type a formula. Syntax only, not a hint: use / for fractions, ^ for powers, and sqrt( ) for square roots, with brackets around a whole numerator or denominator, as in (u^2 + 1)/(3*u) or sqrt(u^2 + 1). The formula appears typeset as you type it, so look at the picture before you submit.

This is a review of a day that has already passed. Use the walk-throughs to go back over the algebra, and to test yourself on any part you skimmed the first time. The proof you write should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 1.2.3

Remove the second-highest term

Problem 1.2.3. a. Find \(M\) so that the substitution \(x = y - M\) depresses the general cubic \(ax^3+bx^2+cx+d=0\), with \(a \ne 0\). Then find \(p\) and \(q\) in terms of \(a\), \(b\), \(c\), \(d\).
b. Find \(K\) so that the substitution \(x = y - K\) depresses the general quartic \(ax^4+bx^3+cx^2+dx+e=0\). Make sure you demonstrate how you obtained that value, or why it works (if you guessed it).
c. Find \(N\) so that the substitution \(x = y - N\) depresses a polynomial of degree \(n\), \(a_nx^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0=0\). Show how you obtained the value, or why it works.

Goal: Find the shift that removes the second-highest power of a cubic, a quartic and any polynomial, and check that it works. It builds on the reading’s shift \(x = y - \frac{b}{2a}\), which removes the \(x\) term of a quadratic. It also sets up the depressed cubic \(y^3 + py + q = 0\), the form Tartaglia already knew how to solve.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 1.2.4

Start from the two roots

Problem 1.2.4. Here is yet another way to solve a quadratic equation. Suppose that \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) are solutions of \(ax^2+bx+c=0\), with \(a>0\) (without loss of generality) and \(r_1 \ge r_2\). Read the development in your reading with pencil and paper handy. Confirm all of the computations that are not completely transparent to you. Then use your notes to present the solution with all steps filled in.

Goal: Derive the Quadratic Formula from the two roots themselves, filling in every step, and say what the derivation assumes. It is a second route to the formula from Lesson Two. It also shows what the formula needs from the number \(b^2 - 4ac\), which the Look back takes up.
