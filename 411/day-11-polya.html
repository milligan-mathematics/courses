How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

Some steps ask for a formula. Where a step asks for a coefficient or an exponent, type it as a formula in the letter the step names, such as n. The page checks your formula at several values, so any form that agrees with the answer everywhere it is checked will pass. Steps that ask for a number accept an exact form like \(\frac{43}{30}\) or ln(2), and e is Euler’s number.

Printed answers. Some of these problems print their answers. The walk-through is about how the answer comes out, and what the series can and can’t be trusted for. Where a step needs a fact the course hasn’t reached yet, the walk-through says so.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.3.1

Integrate the geometric series

Problem 3.3.1. Use the geometric series to obtain the series \(\ln(1+x) = x-\frac12x^2+\frac13x^3-\cdots = \sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{n+1}x^{n+1}\).

Goal: Build a series for \(\ln(1+x)\) from the geometric series, by way of a derivative you can integrate. It is Move 3–4 on the day page, integration term by term, applied to a logarithm. Problem 3.3.2 uses this series again, with \(u = \frac{x}{2}\).

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.3.2

Four series about 0, with no derivatives

Problem 3.3.2. Without using Taylor’s Theorem, represent the following functions as power series expanded about 0 (i.e., in the form \(\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_nx^n\)).
(a) \(\ln\left(1-x^2\right)\)
(b) \(\frac{x}{1+x^2}\)
(c) \(\arctan\left(x^3\right)\)
(d) \(\ln\left(2+x\right)\)
The book adds: \(2+x = 2\left(1+\frac{x}{2}\right)\).

Goal: Find four power series about \(0\) from series you already have, by differentiating, multiplying, substituting or factoring. It uses the day page’s moves together: substitute, multiply through, integrate term by term, and carry the region of validity. Part (d) uses the series for \(\ln(1+u)\) from Problem 3.3.1.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.3.3

A power series for a to the x

Problem 3.3.3. Let \(a\) be a positive real number. Find a power series for \(a^x\) expanded about 0.

Goal: Rewrite \(a^x\) as a power of \(e\), then use the series for \(e^u\) with the right \(u\). It is the exponential series with a change of variable, Move 1–2 on the day page, applied to a new base. The base enters the series only through the number \(\ln a\).

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.3.4

Recentre sin x at a

Problem 3.3.4. Represent the function \(\sin x\) as a power series expanded about \(a\) (i.e., in the form \(\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n\left(x-a\right)^n\)).

Goal: Recentre \(\sin x\) at \(a\): write \(x = (x-a)+a\) and use the addition formula to get two series in \(x-a\). It is Move 5 on the day page, recentring, applied to a function with two pieces. Chapter 4 gets the same coefficients from derivatives, and the check at \(x = a\) is the one the day page uses.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.3.5

Four recentrings

Problem 3.3.5. Without using Taylor’s Theorem, represent the following functions as a power series expanded about \(a\) for the given value of \(a\) (i.e., in the form \(\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n\left(x-a\right)^n\)).
(a) \(\ln x\), \(a=1\)
(b) \(e^x\), \(a=3\)
(c) \(x^3+2x^2+3\), \(a=1\)
(d) \(\frac{1}{x}\), \(a=5\)

Goal: Recentre four functions at the points given, each by the route that fits it: integrate a geometric series, factor out \(e^3\), shift a polynomial, or rewrite \(\frac1x\) as a geometric series. It puts Move 5 on the day page to work on four functions. Part (d) is close to the day page’s \(\frac{1}{3-x}\) demonstration, and part (a) brings integration back in.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 3.3.6

Integrals as series

Problem 3.3.6. Evaluate the following integrals as series.
\(\displaystyle\int_0^1 e^{x^2}\,dx\)
\(\displaystyle\int_0^1\frac{1}{1+x^4}\,dx\)
\(\displaystyle\int_0^1\sqrt[3]{1-x^3}\,dx\)

Goal: Write each integrand as a power series, integrate term by term from \(0\) to \(1\), and say what the answer can be trusted for. It is Move 3–4 on the day page, integration term by term, put to work on integrals that the tools of this chapter can’t evaluate in closed form.
