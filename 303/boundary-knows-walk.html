How this works. Each card follows one Part of the handout. Predict or compute on paper with your group, then answer the matching step here. Every answer gets feedback right away: if something’s off, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

Typing answers. Most answers are multiples of \(\pi\). Type 18pi or 18π (that one is from item 3), and fractions like 3/2 work too.

Paper first. The handout is what you turn in, so keep your work there. This page is for checking it, and for the “Talk it over” questions it shows one good answer after you’ve written yours.

The Boundary Knows · Part A

Two meters for a vector field

Part A. Items 1 and 2 on the handout. Four fields are pictured on page 1: A \(\langle x, y\rangle\), B \(\langle -y, x\rangle\), C \(\langle y, 0\rangle\), D \(\langle x, -y\rangle\). Predict the sign of the divergence \(P_x + Q_y\) and the curl \(Q_x - P_y\) from each picture, then compute. Item 2 uses \(\mathbf G = \nabla(xyz) = \langle yz, xz, xy\rangle\), the field from the end of 15.2.

Goal: Read spreading (divergence) and spin (curl) off a picture, then confirm them with partial derivatives. All three big theorems in Parts B, D and E are built from these two derivatives.

The Boundary Knows · Part B

Green’s Theorem: spin inside, circulation on the edge

Part B. Items 3, 4 and 5 on the handout. Item 3: the swirl \(\langle -y, x\rangle\) around the circle of radius 3, two ways. Item 4: \(\mathbf F = \langle x^2 - y,\ x + e^{y^2}\rangle\) around the square with corners \((0,0), (2,0), (2,2), (0,2)\). Item 5 (starred): \(\oint_C x\,dy\) around the ellipse \(x = 3\cos t\), \(y = 2\sin t\). Then the Talk it over box on fluxes.

Goal: See why total spin inside equals circulation around the edge, and use it when the edge is the hard side. Stokes (Part D) is this same theorem with the flat region bent into a surface.

The Boundary Knows · Part C

Integrating over a surface

Part C. Items 6 and 7 on the handout. The side of a can of radius 3 and height 2: \(\mathbf r(\theta, z) = \langle 3\cos\theta,\ 3\sin\theta,\ z\rangle\), \(0 \le \theta \le 2\pi\), \(0 \le z \le 2\). Then three fluxes: rain \(\langle 0, 0, -1\rangle\) through the side and through the lid, and a sprinkler \(\langle x, y, 0\rangle\) through the side.

Goal: Use one cross product to get both a surface’s normal and its area factor, then compute fluxes with it. Stokes and the Divergence Theorem (Parts D and E) both have a surface integral on one side.

The Boundary Knows · Part D

Stokes’ Theorem: the butterfly net

Part D. Item 8 on the handout. \(\mathbf F = \langle -y,\ x,\ xz\rangle\), and \(C\) is the unit circle in the \(xy\)-plane, counterclockwise seen from above. Cap \(C\) with the flat disk \(S_1\), then with the dome \(S_2\) (the upper half of the unit sphere). Then the starred Talk it over box.

Goal: See that every surface hung from the same rim catches the same flux of curl. It explains the 15.3 component test, and Part E does the same for a closed surface.

The Boundary Knows · Part E

The Divergence Theorem, and the whole family

Part E. Items 9, 10 and 11 on the handout. Item 9: \(\mathbf F = \langle x, y, z\rangle\) out of the sphere of radius 2, two ways. Item 10 (starred): the sprinkler \(\langle x, y, 0\rangle\) out of the closed can from item 6 (radius 3, height 2, lid and bottom on). Item 11: the family portrait, then the Talk it over box and the exit ticket.

Goal: Trade a flux through a closed surface for a volume integral, then see all six theorems as one idea. This finishes the trailer: 15.8 is the last section of the book.
