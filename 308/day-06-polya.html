How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.1

Five claims, each proved or broken

Exercise 4.5.1. Prove or disprove each of the following statements.
(a) All of the generators of \(\mathbb Z_{60}\) are prime.
(b) \(U(8)\) is cyclic.
(c) \(\mathbb Q\) is cyclic.
(d) If every proper subgroup of a group \(G\) is cyclic, then \(G\) is a cyclic group.
(e) A group with a finite number of subgroups is finite.

Goal: Decide each claim, then back it with one counterexample or a proof. Part (a) shows the method; the later parts are yours to do with less help. It uses the definitions of a cyclic subgroup, a generator and the order of an element from Section 4.1, and Example 4.1.7 for part (d). Theorem 4.1.9 (cyclic groups are abelian) goes one way only: \(U(8)\) and \(\mathbb Q\) are abelian groups that are not cyclic.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.2

Six elements, six ways to come back to the identity

Exercise 4.5.2. Find the order of each of the following elements.
(a) \(5 \in \mathbb Z_{12}\)
(b) \(\sqrt3 \in \mathbb R\)
(c) \(\sqrt3 \in \mathbb R^\ast\)
(d) \(-i \in \mathbb C^\ast\)
(e) \(72 \in \mathbb Z_{240}\)
(f) \(312 \in \mathbb Z_{471}\)

Goal: Find orders by listing multiples, then with a shortcut that you prove in part (e) and reuse in part (f). The same definition works in every group; only the identity changes. It uses the definition of order and of \(\langle a \rangle\) from Section 4.1, the groups \(\mathbb R^\ast\) and \(\mathbb C^\ast\) from Chapter 3, and Corollary 2.2.3 (\(ar + bs = 1\) for relatively prime \(a\) and \(b\)) in parts (e) and (f).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.5

Every order in Z_18, from one shortcut

Exercise 4.5.5. Find the order of every element in \(\mathbb Z_{18}\).

Goal: Find the order of each element of \(\mathbb Z_{18}\) with the shortcut from Exercise 4.5.2, then check your table with two counts. It uses the definition of order from Section 4.1 and the shortcut argued in Exercise 4.5.2(e), which works for any element of any \(\mathbb Z_n\).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.6

Rotations count turns, flips undo themselves

Exercise 4.5.6. Find the order of every element in the symmetry group of the square, \(D_4\).

Goal: Sort the eight symmetries into rotations and flips, and find each order from the definition: count turns for the rotations, and ask what a flip does when you repeat it. It uses the definition of order from Section 4.1 and the symmetry groups from Chapter 3. It is the last problem today, so it ends in a written explanation.
