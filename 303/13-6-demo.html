Zoom Until It's Flat

§13.6 — magnify a surface until it looks like its tangent plane, then see how well that plane predicts the change in height.

Pick a function first. In panel 1, the gray sheet is the surface z = f(x, y) and the green sheet is its tangent plane at the black point (a, b). Drag the zoom slider to magnify the picture around that point, and watch the surface flatten into the plane. In panel 2, move Δx and Δy to step from (a, b) to a new point. The orange curve is the true height along that step, the blue line is the plane's height, and the two dots mark both heights at the new point.

What you are seeing

The picture squeezes the heights to fit the screen, so the surface looks flatter than it really is. The readouts always use the true heights. At large zoom the gray surface and the green plane become one sheet, because a smooth surface looks flat up close.

Things to try

  1. Predict first. Choose cos x + y². Before you touch the sliders, predict whether the plane over- or under-estimates the height after a step of Δx = 0.2 and Δy = −0.8. Then set those two values and check the readout.
  2. Halve the step. Predict how much the error shrinks when you halve both Δx and Δy: by a factor of 2, 4, or 8? Make one prediction for the error in height and another for the percent error. Then check both readouts.
  3. Bowl or dome. Choose the ex+y bowl, which curves up in every direction, and predict whether the plane over- or under-estimates a step of Δx = Δy = 0.5. Then choose the dome, which curves down, and predict again for the same step.
  4. Saddle-like. Choose cos x + y². With Δy = 0, predict whether the plane over- or under-estimates for Δx = 0.5. Then set Δx = 0 and Δy = 0.5 and predict again. Why do the two answers differ?
  5. Predict the zoom. Choose the linearization example and zoom in slowly. Before you look, predict the zoom at which the surface first looks flat to you. Then zoom past ×10 and check whether the largest-gap readout drops in proportion to the window: halving the window should halve the gap.
