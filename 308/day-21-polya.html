How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.1

The determinant multiplies

Exercise 11.4.1. Prove that \(\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B)\) for \(A, B \in GL_2(\mathbb R)\). This shows that the determinant is a homomorphism from \(GL_2(\mathbb R)\) to \(\mathbb R^\ast\).

Goal: Prove an identity about products by multiplying out with general entries, and see why it makes the determinant a homomorphism into \(\mathbb R^\ast\). It uses the \(2 \times 2\) determinant and the definition of a homomorphism from Section 11.1. Exercise 11.4.2(d) uses this map, so the work here carries over.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.2

Which maps keep the operation?

Exercise 11.4.2. Which of the following maps are homomorphisms? If the map is a homomorphism, what is the kernel?
(a) \(\phi : \mathbb R^\ast \to GL_2(\mathbb R)\) defined by \(\phi(a) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & a \end{pmatrix}\)
(b) \(\phi : \mathbb R \to GL_2(\mathbb R)\) defined by \(\phi(a) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ a & 1 \end{pmatrix}\)
(c) \(\phi : GL_2(\mathbb R) \to \mathbb R\) defined by \(\phi\left(\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}\right) = a + d\)
(d) \(\phi : GL_2(\mathbb R) \to \mathbb R^\ast\) defined by \(\phi\left(\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}\right) = ad - bc\)
(e) \(\phi : M_2(\mathbb R) \to \mathbb R\) defined by \(\phi\left(\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}\right) = b\), where \(M_2(\mathbb R)\) is the additive group of \(2 \times 2\) matrices with entries in \(\mathbb R\).

Goal: Test a map by the one condition that defines a homomorphism, find kernels, and see how a small change in a formula breaks the test. It uses the definition from Section 11.1 and the kernel from Theorem 11.1.5. Part (d) is the determinant from Exercise 11.4.1, so that walk-through comes first.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.3

Matrices respect addition

Exercise 11.4.3. Let \(A\) be an \(m \times n\) matrix. Show that matrix multiplication, \(x \mapsto Ax\), defines a homomorphism \(\phi : \mathbb R^n \to \mathbb R^m\).

Goal: Check the homomorphism test for a map given by a formula, and find the one property of matrix multiplication that does the work. It uses the definition from Section 11.1 and the distributive law for matrices. Both \(\mathbb R^n\) and \(\mathbb R^m\) are groups under vector addition.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.4

Multiply by seven

Exercise 11.4.4. Let \(\phi : \mathbb Z \to \mathbb Z\) be given by \(\phi(n) = 7n\). Prove that \(\phi\) is a group homomorphism. Find the kernel and the image of \(\phi\).

Goal: Prove the homomorphism test for every pair of integers at once, then read the kernel and the image off the formula. It uses the definitions of kernel and image from Section 11.1 and the distributive law in \(\mathbb Z\). The kernel is normal by Theorem 11.1.5.
