How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

A correction to the reading. As printed in the reading, Problem 8.4.14 leaves out one hypothesis, that \(f^\prime\) is continuous on \((a,b)\), which Corollary 8.4.12 has. Without it the statement is false. The walk-through adds the hypothesis and shows you why it is needed.

The walk-throughs end in a full proof, so the proof you bring to class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.4.3

Differentiable means continuous

Problem 8.4.3. Prove Theorem 8.4.2: If \(f\) is differentiable at a point \(c\), then \(f\) is continuous at \(c\) as well.

Goal: Turn a statement about the difference quotient into a statement about \(f(x)\) itself: write \(f(x)-f(c)\) as the quotient times \(x-c\), and let the limits do the work. It is the first theorem of Section 8.4, and it supplies the continuity that the MVT needs in Problems 8.4.11 and 8.4.13.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.4.5

Finish Fermat from the left

Problem 8.4.5. Theorem 8.4.4 (Fermat’s Theorem): suppose \(f\) is differentiable in an interval \((a,b)\) containing \(c\). If \(f(c)\ge f(x)\) for every \(x\) in \((a,b)\), then \(f^\prime(c)=0\). Problem 8.4.5 asks you to complete the proof: show that \(f^\prime(c)\ge 0\), and conclude that \(f^\prime(c)=0\).

Goal: Finish Fermat’s Theorem. The reading proved \(f^\prime(c)\le 0\), and this problem supplies the other inequality. It is the other half of the proof you read. Problem 8.4.6 uses the same theorem for a minimum.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.4.6

Minima are flat too

Problem 8.4.6. Problem 8.4.6: Show Fermat’s Theorem holds for local minima. Show that if \(f(c)\le f(x)\) for all \(x\) in some interval \((a,b)\), then \(f^\prime(c)=0\) too. (Assumed, as in Theorem 8.4.4: \(c\in(a,b)\) and \(f\) is differentiable on \((a,b)\).)

Goal: Use the maximum case you already have, applied to a different function, and track what happens to the derivative. Problem 8.4.5 finished Fermat for maxima. This problem covers minima with the same theorem, and the trick of replacing \(f\) by \(-f\) comes back in Problem 8.4.14.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.4.9

Tilt the secant, then apply Rolle

Problem 8.4.9. Prove the Mean Value Theorem (Theorem 8.4.7) using Rolle’s Theorem (Theorem 8.4.8). The MVT says: suppose \(f^\prime\) exists for every \(x\in(a,b)\) and \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\). Then there is a real number \(c\in(a,b)\) such that \(f^\prime(c)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\).

Goal: Find a function that Rolle’s Theorem can be applied to, and then read the MVT off its derivative. Rolle’s Theorem is the special case \(f(a)=f(b)\). The MVT carries the rest of the section: Corollaries 8.4.10 and 8.4.12, and Problems 8.4.11 and 8.4.13, all use it.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.4.11

Falling slope, falling graph

Problem 8.4.11. Show that if \(f^\prime(x)<0\) for every \(x\) in the interval \((a,b)\), then \(f\) is decreasing on \((a,b)\) (strictly, as in Corollary 8.4.10).

Goal: Turn a sign of \(f^\prime\) into a statement about values of \(f\), one pair of points at a time. It is the mirror image of Corollary 8.4.10, which you can use as a model. Problem 8.4.13 asks for the local version: a sign of \(f^\prime\) at one point gives a rising interval.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.4.13

Positive at a point, positive nearby

Problem 8.4.13. Prove Corollary 8.4.12. Suppose \(f\) is differentiable on some interval \((a,b)\), \(f^\prime\) is continuous on \((a,b)\), and \(f^\prime(c)>0\) for some \(c\in(a,b)\). Then there is an interval \(I\subset(a,b)\) containing \(c\) such that for every \(x,y\in I\) with \(x\ge y\), \(f(x)\ge f(y)\).

Goal: Go from one point to an interval. The hypothesis is about \(f^\prime\) at a single point, and the conclusion is about values of \(f\) on an interval. This is the local version of Corollary 8.4.10. Its extra hypothesis, continuity of \(f^\prime\), is where the proof needs more than differentiability. Problem 8.4.14 is its mirror image.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.4.14

Negative at a point, falling nearby

Problem 8.4.14. Show that if \(f\) is differentiable on some interval \((a,b)\), \(f^\prime\) is continuous on \((a,b)\), and \(f^\prime(c)<0\) for some \(c\in(a,b)\), then there is an interval \(I\subset(a,b)\) containing \(c\) such that for every \(x,y\in I\) with \(x\ge y\), \(f(x)\le f(y)\). (The continuity of \(f^\prime\) is part of the hypothesis here, as in Corollary 8.4.12. It is missing from the problem as printed in the reading. The Look back shows a counterexample without it.)

Goal: Mirror the proof of Problem 8.4.13 with the inequalities reversed, and find out which hypothesis carries the weight. It is the mirror image of Problem 8.4.13. The proof leans on the continuity of \(f^\prime\), and the Look back shows what happens without it.
