Chapter 1 · A

Why do mathematics?

A. Chapter 1, “Flourishing.” Su opens with two people, Christopher Jackson and Simone Weil, and a line from Weil: “Every being cries out silently to be read differently.” Then he asks the question the whole book answers: Why do mathematics?

Goal: Follow Su’s answer to “Why do mathematics?”, then decide how far you agree. Each later chapter takes one human desire (play, beauty, truth, struggle, and so on) and the virtues it builds. Your Math & Faith paper will draw on these.

Chapter 1 · Puzzle

Toggling Light Switches

Puzzle. 100 lightbulbs in a row, numbered 1 to 100, all off. Toggle every switch that’s a multiple of 1, then every multiple of 2, then every multiple of 3, and so on up to multiples of 100. (Toggling flips a switch: off to on, on to off.) When you’re done, which bulbs are on? Do you see a pattern? Can you explain it? (Su, end of Chapter 1.)

Goal: Find which bulbs end up on, and explain why, not just observe it. The pairing trick here (each divisor \(d\) of \(n\) has a partner \(n/d\)) is the same one behind counting divisors in number theory.

Chapter 1 · Puzzle

Dividing Brownies

Puzzle. A father bakes brownies in a rectangular pan for his two daughters. Before they get home, his wife cuts out a rectangle from somewhere in the middle, its sides not necessarily parallel to the sides of the pan. How can he make one straight cut that divides the rest evenly, so the daughters get the same area? (Su, end of Chapter 1.)

Goal: Find one straight cut that always works, and say why it works. Point symmetry (a figure that looks the same turned 180° about its center) turns up all through geometry, and it’s the whole key here.
