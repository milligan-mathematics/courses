How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.8

Powers that respect products

Exercise 11.4.8. If \(G\) is an abelian group and \(n \in \mathbb N\), show that \(\phi : G \rightarrow G\) defined by \(g \mapsto g^n\) is a group homomorphism.

Goal: Check the homomorphism condition for a map given by a formula, and find the one hypothesis that the check needs. It uses the definition of a homomorphism from Section 11.1 (Day 21). The same product check is what Exercises 9 and 10 use on the image.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.9

The image inherits commutativity

Exercise 11.4.9. If \(\phi : G \rightarrow H\) is a group homomorphism and \(G\) is abelian, prove that \(\phi(G)\) is also abelian.

Goal: Find which group the commuting has to happen in, and push it through \(\phi\) using only the product rule. It uses the homomorphism property from Section 11.1 (Day 21) and the image \(\phi(G)\), which is a subgroup of \(H\) by Proposition 11.1.4. Exercise 10 asks the same question about cyclic groups.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.10

The image of a cyclic group

Exercise 11.4.10. If \(\phi : G \rightarrow H\) is a group homomorphism and \(G\) is cyclic, prove that \(\phi(G)\) is also cyclic.

Goal: Name a generator of \(G\) first, then follow an arbitrary element of \(\phi(G)\) back to \(G\) and forward again. It uses the homomorphism property and the powers of a generator from Chapter 4, and it sets up the First Isomorphism Theorem from Day 22, where the image of \(G\) is \(G\) modulo its kernel.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.18

One-to-one from the kernel

Exercise 11.4.18. Let \(\phi : G \rightarrow H\) be a group homomorphism. Show that \(\phi\) is one-to-one if and only if \(\phi^{-1}(e) = \{ e \}\).

Goal: Prove an “if and only if” one direction at a time, and see exactly where the homomorphism property does its work. It uses the kernel from Section 11.1 (Day 21) and Proposition 11.1.4 (identities and inverses). The First Isomorphism Theorem from Day 22 is the same idea used in a larger setting.
