How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 10.4.10

Two cosets, and a normal subgroup

Exercise 10.4.10. Let \(H\) be a subgroup of index \(2\) of a group \(G\). Prove that \(H\) must be a normal subgroup of \(G\). Conclude that \(S_n\) is not simple for \(n \geq 3\).

Goal: Show that a subgroup with only two cosets has the same left and right cosets, then find a normal subgroup of \(S_n\) that is neither trivial nor all of \(S_n\). It uses the coset count (Theorem 6.1.8: as many left cosets as right cosets), and the even permutations: Proposition 5.1.17 and Lagrange’s Theorem (Theorem 6.2.2) give \([S_n : A_n] = 2\). Normal means \(gH = Hg\) for every \(g\), as in Section 10.1.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 10.4.11

One subgroup of its order

Exercise 10.4.11. If a group \(G\) has exactly one subgroup \(H\) of order \(k\), prove that \(H\) is normal in \(G\).

Goal: Show that conjugating \(H\) by any \(g\) gives another subgroup of order \(k\), then use uniqueness to say it is \(H\) itself. It uses the definition of normal from Section 10.1 and the cancellation laws (Proposition 3.2.15). The only hypothesis is uniqueness, so the proof has to get everything from it.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 10.4.13

The center, and what a cyclic quotient forces

Exercise 10.4.13. Recall that the center of a group \(G\) is the set \(Z(G) = \{ x \in G : xg = gx \text{ for all } g \in G \}\).
(a) Calculate the center of \(S_3\).
(b) Calculate the center of \(GL_2(\mathbb R)\).
(c) Show that the center of any group \(G\) is a normal subgroup of \(G\).
(d) If \(G/Z(G)\) is cyclic, show that \(G\) is abelian.

Goal: Find two centers by testing each element against the others, then prove two general facts: that \(Z(G)\) is normal, and that a cyclic \(G/Z(G)\) forces \(G\) to be abelian. It uses factor groups (Section 10.1), the group \(GL_2(\mathbb R)\) of Example 3.2.7, and the definition of the center in the exercise. The walk-through checks that \(Z(G)\) is a subgroup, so you don’t need to look that up.
