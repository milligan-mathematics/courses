How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 6.5.6

Sorting matrices by determinant

Exercise 6.5.6. Describe the left cosets of \(SL_2(\mathbb R)\) in \(GL_2(\mathbb R)\). What is the index of \(SL_2(\mathbb R)\) in \(GL_2(\mathbb R)\)?

Goal: See what a left coset looks like inside a group of matrices, and find the one number that sorts the matrices into cosets. It uses the definition of a left coset and the index from Section 6.1, and the test for when two cosets are equal (Lemma 6.1.3). Lagrange’s Theorem (Theorem 6.2.2) is the tool that doesn’t apply here, and the Look back says why.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 6.5.9

Infinitely many cosets of the integers

Exercise 6.5.9. Show that the integers have infinite index in the additive group of rational numbers.

Goal: See that a subgroup can be infinite and still leave infinitely many cosets, and that the index counts cosets, not elements. It uses the index from Section 6.1 and the test for when two cosets are equal (Lemma 6.1.3, written additively). Lagrange’s Theorem can’t help here, because \(\mathbb Q\) isn’t finite.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 6.5.12

Left cosets that match right cosets

Exercise 6.5.12. If \(ghg^{-1} \in H\) for all \(g \in G\) and \(h \in H\), show that right cosets are identical to left cosets. That is, show that \(gH = Hg\) for all \(g \in G\).

Goal: Turn a hypothesis about \(ghg^{-1}\) into a statement about cosets, and see that both inclusions come from one fact. It uses left and right cosets from Section 6.1 (Remark 6.1.5 says right cosets partition \(G\) too), and the example \(S_3\) from Example 6.1.7. The hypothesis is a condition you will meet again, and this exercise shows what it buys.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 6.5.14

Powers of g that return to e

Exercise 6.5.14. Suppose that \(g^n = e\). Show that the order of \(g\) divides \(n\).

Goal: Use the definition of order and the division algorithm to show that every exponent that sends \(g\) to \(e\) is a multiple of \(|g|\). It uses the definition of order from Section 4.1, the division algorithm from Chapter 2, and it is Proposition 4.1.12 for the cyclic group \(\langle g \rangle\). Corollary 6.2.3, the corollary of Lagrange’s Theorem, is the wrong tool: it bounds \(|g|\) by the size of a finite group, and says nothing about \(n\).
