How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 10.4.4

Triangular matrices and a normal subgroup

Exercise 10.4.4. Let \(T\) be the group of nonsingular upper triangular \(2 \times 2\) matrices with entries in \(\mathbb R\); that is, matrices \(\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & c \end{pmatrix}\), where \(a, b, c \in \mathbb R\) and \(ac \neq 0\). Let \(U\) consist of matrices of the form \(\begin{pmatrix} 1 & x \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\), where \(x \in \mathbb R\).
(a) Show that \(U\) is a subgroup of \(T\).
(b) Prove that \(U\) is abelian.
(c) Prove that \(U\) is normal in \(T\).
(d) Show that \(T/U\) is abelian.
(e) Is \(T\) normal in \(GL_2(\mathbb R)\)?

Goal: Check a subgroup with the subgroup test, decide normality by conjugating rather than by guessing, and see why the quotient is abelian even when the whole group is not. It uses the subgroup test (Proposition 3.3.8), the conjugation test for normality (Theorem 10.1.3), and the product rule for cosets (Theorem 10.1.4). Part (e) is the case where normality fails.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 10.4.6

Quotients of abelian groups

Exercise 10.4.6. If \(G\) is abelian, prove that \(G/H\) must also be abelian.

Goal: Turn a claim about all pairs of cosets into a claim about pairs of elements of \(G\), where the hypothesis applies. It uses the product rule \((aH)(bH) = abH\) from Theorem 10.1.4, and Example 10.1.1: every subgroup of an abelian group is normal, so \(G/H\) exists.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 10.4.7

Abelian pieces, nonabelian whole

Exercise 10.4.7. Prove or disprove: If \(H\) is a normal subgroup of \(G\) such that \(H\) and \(G/H\) are abelian, then \(G\) is abelian.

Goal: Decide whether the statement is true before you try to prove it. To disprove it, one example that meets every hypothesis and breaks the conclusion is enough. It uses normality (Theorem 10.1.3), the order of a factor group (Theorem 10.1.4), Corollary 6.2.4 (a group of prime order is cyclic), Example 10.1.2, which shows that \(A_3\) is normal in \(S_3\), and Example 10.1.5, the factor group \(S_3/A_3\).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 10.4.8

Cyclic quotients

Exercise 10.4.8. If \(G\) is cyclic, prove that \(G/H\) must also be cyclic.

Goal: Find one coset whose powers reach every coset of \(H\). Then write the proof so that each coset is visibly a power of it. It uses the definition of a cyclic group, the product rule of Theorem 10.1.4, and Corollary 4.1.14 for the generators of \(\mathbb Z_n\). Exercise 10.4.9 asks about the converse.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 10.4.9

A cyclic subgroup and a cyclic quotient

Exercise 10.4.9. Prove or disprove: If \(H\) and \(G/H\) are cyclic, then \(G\) is cyclic.

Goal: Look for a counterexample first. If \(H\) and \(G/H\) are both cyclic, does \(G\) have to be, and which fact about \(S_3\) settles it? It uses the definition of cyclic, Theorem 4.1.9 (cyclic groups are abelian), Example 10.1.2 for the normal subgroup \(A_3\) and for why \(\{(1), (1\,2)\}\) can’t replace it, and direct products (Section 9.2) for the variations at the end.
