How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.34

Conjugation keeps the group

Exercise 9.4.34. An automorphism of a group \(G\) is an isomorphism with itself. Prove that complex conjugation is an automorphism of the additive group of complex numbers; that is, show that the map \(\varphi( a + bi ) = a - bi\) is an isomorphism from \(\mathbb C\) to \(\mathbb C\).

Goal: Check the three parts of an isomorphism (one-to-one, onto, and respecting the operation) for a map you can picture, and see which computation covers which part. It uses the definition of isomorphism from Section 9.1: a one-to-one, onto map that respects the operation. The next exercises in this set use the same three checks.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.37

Automorphisms under composition

Exercise 9.4.37. We will denote the set of all automorphisms of \(G\) by \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). Prove that \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) is a subgroup of \(S_G\), the group of permutations of \(G\).

Goal: Apply the subgroup test to a set of maps, and see which of its conditions each part of an automorphism supplies. It uses the subgroup test (Proposition 3.3.7: the identity, closure under the operation, and closure under inverses), the definition of isomorphism from Section 9.1, and Theorem 9.1.6(1): the inverse of an isomorphism is an isomorphism.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.38

The automorphisms of Z6

Exercise 9.4.38. Find \(\operatorname{Aut}( {\mathbb Z}_6)\).

Goal: Find every isomorphism from \(\mathbb Z_6\) to itself by finding what each one must do to \(1\), then test the candidates. It uses the definition of isomorphism (Section 9.1), and the generators of \(\mathbb Z_6\): by Corollary 4.1.14 they are the \(r\) with \(\gcd(r, 6) = 1\), namely \(1\) and \(5\).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.41

Conjugation is an automorphism

Exercise 9.4.41. Let \(G\) be a group and \(g \in G\). Define a map \(i_g : G \rightarrow G\) by \(i_g(x) = g x g^{-1}\). Prove that \(i_g\) defines an automorphism of \(G\). Such an automorphism is called an inner automorphism. The set of all inner automorphisms is denoted by \(\operatorname{Inn}(G)\).

Goal: Show that conjugation by a fixed element respects products, and that it can be undone, for any group \(G\). It uses the definition of isomorphism from Section 9.1: a one-to-one, onto map that respects the operation. A map with an inverse function is automatically one-to-one and onto.
