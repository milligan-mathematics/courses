How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.5

Homomorphisms from Z24 to Z18

Exercise 11.4.5. Describe all of the homomorphisms from \(\mathbb Z_{24}\) to \(\mathbb Z_{18}\).

Goal: Find every homomorphism from \(\mathbb Z_{24}\) to \(\mathbb Z_{18}\) by asking what happens to the generator \(1\), and check the one condition that matters. It uses the definition of a homomorphism (Section 11.1), the fact that a homomorphism sends the identity to the identity (Proposition 11.1.4), and the kernel as a subgroup (Theorem 11.1.5). The look-back uses the First Isomorphism Theorem (Theorem 11.2.1).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.6

Homomorphisms from the integers to Z12

Exercise 11.4.6. Describe all of the homomorphisms from \(\mathbb Z\) to \(\mathbb Z_{12}\).

Goal: Find every homomorphism from the integers to \(\mathbb Z_{12}\), and see why there are more of them here than in Exercise 11.4.5. It uses the same one-value idea as Exercise 11.4.5, and the fact that the identity goes to the identity (Proposition 11.1.4). The difference is that \(\mathbb Z\) has no relation like \(24 = 0\) to check.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.7

Sums and intersections in Z24

Exercise 11.4.7. In the group \(\mathbb Z_{24}\), let \(H = \langle 4 \rangle\) and \(N = \langle 6 \rangle\).
(a) List the elements in \(HN\) (we usually write \(H + N\) for these additive groups) and \(H \cap N\).
(b) List the cosets in \(HN/N\), showing the elements in each coset.
(c) List the cosets in \(H/(H \cap N)\), showing the elements in each coset.
(d) Give the correspondence between \(HN/N\) and \(H/(H \cap N)\) described in the proof of the Second Isomorphism Theorem.

Goal: Find a sum and an intersection of two subgroups, list the cosets in each quotient, and read the correspondence off the map that the proof uses. It uses cosets and their counting (Section 6.1), factor groups (Section 10.1), and the First Isomorphism Theorem (Theorem 11.2.1). Part (d) names the Second Isomorphism Theorem, which you meet in Day 23. You don’t need it here: the walk-through finds the kernel of the map \(h \mapsto h + N\) and applies the First Isomorphism Theorem, which is how that theorem is proved.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 11.4.13

Is Q/Z isomorphic to Q?

Exercise 11.4.13. Prove or disprove: \(\mathbb Q / \mathbb Z \cong \mathbb Q\).

Goal: Decide whether two groups can be isomorphic by finding a property that an isomorphism must keep, then write the argument out. It uses the factor group \(\mathbb Q / \mathbb Z\) (Section 10.1, with \(\mathbb Z\) normal in \(\mathbb Q\) because \(\mathbb Q\) is abelian, Example 10.1.1), and the order of an element. The reading doesn’t state that an isomorphism keeps the order of each element, so the write step proves what it needs for the coset \(\tfrac13 + \mathbb Z\) directly.
