How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 16.7.4

Ideals in five rings

Exercise 16.7.4. Find all of the ideals in each of the following rings. Which of these ideals are maximal and which are prime?
(a) \(\mathbb Z_{18}\)
(b) \(\mathbb Z_{25}\)
(c) \(\mathbb M_2(\mathbb R)\), the \(2 \times 2\) matrices with entries in \(\mathbb R\)
(d) \(\mathbb M_2(\mathbb Z)\), the \(2 \times 2\) matrices with entries in \(\mathbb Z\)
(e) \(\mathbb Q\)

Goal: List every ideal of each ring, and for each ideal say whether it is maximal and whether it is prime, with a reason that uses the quotient ring when the theorems apply and a direct argument when they do not. It uses the definition of an ideal (Section 16.3), Theorem 16.4.1 (a maximal ideal has a field as quotient), Proposition 16.4.4 (a prime ideal has an integral domain as quotient) and Corollary 16.4.6. Examples 16.4.3 and 16.4.5 do the same job for \(\mathbb Z_{12}\) and \(\mathbb Z\).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 16.7.5

Tables for two quotient rings

Exercise 16.7.5. For each of the following rings \(R\) with ideal \(I\), give an addition table and a multiplication table for \(R/I\).
(a) \(R = \mathbb Z\) and \(I = 6\mathbb Z\)
(b) \(R = \mathbb Z_{12}\) and \(I = \{0, 3, 6, 9\}\)

Goal: Write out the addition and multiplication tables of a quotient ring, say why the entries are well defined, and recognize each quotient as a ring you already know. It uses the factor ring \(R/I\) of Theorem 16.3.10 and the cosets from Chapter 6. Theorem 16.3.12 (the First Isomorphism Theorem) explains why both quotients match a \(\mathbb Z_n\); Theorem 16.4.1 and Proposition 16.4.4 turn the answers into statements about maximal and prime ideals.
