Field Flow

§15.1 — pick a vector field, drag a probe to read the vector at any point, and release particles that flow along the arrows.

Each arrow is the vector F(x, y) = ⟨P, Q⟩ drawn with its tail at its point. Drag the blue probe (or type its coordinates) to read the vector there. Click an empty spot on the picture, or press Release a particle at the probe, to start a particle. A particle moves so that its velocity is the field at its position, so it follows the arrows. The last two readouts try the component test ∂P/∂y = ∂Q/∂x at the probe and over the whole picture.

The picture shows −3 ≤ x, y ≤ 3. Where a field is not defined (the center of the inverse-square and outflow fields) nothing is drawn, and a particle that reaches it is removed. The gravity field here is the slice z = 0 of the section page’s gravitational field with GMm = 1.

Things to try

  1. Pick the swirl F = ⟨−y, x⟩ (the first example on the section page). Before you release anything, predict which way a particle started at (2, 0) will go and whether it speeds up or slows down as it goes around. Release it and check. Then read the length at (1, 0) and at (2, 0).
  2. Pick the outflow field ⟨x, y⟩/(2∥r∥). Predict the length readout at three very different probe points, then check them. Why do all the arrows have the same length? Tick “same length” and see what changes (and what doesn’t).
  3. Pick the gravity slice. Put the probe at distance 1 from the center, read the length, then predict the length at distance 2 and at distance 3 before you move the probe. How do the readouts compare with the inverse-square law?
  4. Pick the gradient field ⟨2x + cos y, −x sin y⟩. Move the probe anywhere and compare the two component-test numbers. Then pick the swirl and do the same. Which one will pass everywhere, and which one fails everywhere?
  5. Pick the spiral ⟨x − y, x + y⟩. It is a swirl plus an outflow. Predict the path of a particle before you release it, then release two particles at different distances. For the pendulum field ⟨y, −sin x⟩, do the particles near the origin behave differently from the ones near (3, 0)?
