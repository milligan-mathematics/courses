Things to try

Predict first, then check with the demo.

  1. Egg carton: predict every point. Look only at the contour map. Before you read the table, predict whether each numbered point is a peak, a valley, or a saddle. Then check. Which prediction was hardest?
  2. Quartic x⁴ − y⁴: find where the test says nothing. Find the one critical point. Before you read its row, predict what the surface does along the two axes. Then read the row: what does \(D\) say, and what can the test conclude?
  3. Cross-term quartic: predict all three. Predict the type of each point from the contour map, then click each one to check. What happens to \(f\) when you replace \((x,y)\) by \((-x,-y)\)?
  4. Five points: connect to the second worked example. This is the function from the second worked example on the section page. Predict the type of \((\tfrac12, \tfrac32)\) from the contour map. Then check \(f_{xx}\) and \(D\). Did the sign of \(f_{xx}\) agree with your prediction?
  5. Away from the critical points. Drag the probe to a spot where the arrow is long, and read \(D\) there. Why can that sign not tell you whether a peak or a valley is nearby?
