How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 13.4.2

All abelian groups of order 200

Exercise 13.4.2. Find all of the abelian groups of order \(200\) up to isomorphism.

Goal: List every abelian group of order \(200\) once, with no repeats, and be able to say why the list is complete and why no two entries are the same group. It uses the Fundamental Theorem of Finite Abelian Groups (Theorem 13.1.4), direct products from Chapter 9, and element orders from Chapter 4. Example 13.1.5 does the same job for order \(540\).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 13.4.3

All abelian groups of order 720

Exercise 13.4.3. Find all of the abelian groups of order \(720\) up to isomorphism.

Goal: Count and list the abelian groups of order \(720\), using one choice per prime, and see that two groups with the same order and the same largest element order can still differ. It uses the Fundamental Theorem of Finite Abelian Groups (Theorem 13.1.4), the method of Example 13.1.5, and element orders in direct products. It is Exercise 13.4.2 with three primes.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 13.4.4

Composition series of eight groups

Exercise 13.4.4. Find all of the composition series for each of the following groups.
(a) \(\mathbb Z_{12}\)
(b) \(\mathbb Z_{48}\)
(c) The quaternions, \(Q_8\)
(d) \(D_4\)
(e) \(S_3 \times \mathbb Z_4\)
(f) \(S_4\)
(g) \(S_n\), \(n \geq 5\)
(h) \(\mathbb Q\)

Goal: For each group, find every chain of subgroups from the whole group down to the trivial group in which each step is a maximal normal subgroup, and see what the Jordan–Hölder Theorem does and does not say about the chains. It uses normal subgroups and factor groups (Chapter 10), simple groups, subnormal series and composition series (Section 13.2), the Jordan–Hölder Theorem (Theorem 13.2.8), and the series Examples 13.2.5 and 13.2.6.
