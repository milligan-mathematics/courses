Slicing for Partials

§13.3 — put a point on a surface, slice it with the planes y = b and x = a, and watch the slopes of the two slices become the partial derivatives fx and fy.

Each surface is z = f(x, y). Put the point (a, b) on it with the sliders, or drag on the small top-down map. The orange x-trace is the slice by the plane y = b, and the orange y-trace is the slice by x = a. Each trace has a blue tangent line at the point. The readout gives f(a, b), fx(a, b), and fy(a, b) from exact formulas. Drag inside the 3D view to orbit it.

Things to try

Each one asks you to predict first, then check with the demo.

  1. Ridge, point at (1, 1). Before you read the readout, look at the two orange traces through the point and predict the signs of fx(1, 1) and fy(1, 1). Then check. The first worked example on the section page finds fx and fy by hand for a different function and builds the blue tangent lines from them. Here the blue lines are the same objects.
  2. Twisted saddle, point at (1, 2). The orange x-trace at b = 2 is a straight line (its height is x sin 2 there), so its blue tangent line lies exactly on top of it. Predict from that line whether fx(1, 2) is positive or negative, then predict the sign of fy(1, 2) from the y-trace. Check both. The function x sin y is the first term of the function in the mixed-partials worked example on the page.
  3. Find fx = 0 with fy ≠ 0. On the Ridge preset, first set b = 0. Then slide a until the orange x-trace looks perfectly flat at its top, near a = 0.7. Read fx: it should be very close to 0. Now move b away from 0 while a stays near 0.7. Is fx still close to 0? Is fy still 0? Explain from the picture why fy is not.
  4. Ripples: does fx depend on b? Choose Ripples and keep the x-trace on. With a fixed, predict whether fx(a, b) changes as you slide b from one end to the other. Check the readout, then explain your answer using the formula for fx.
  5. Stretched hill, point at (0.5, 0.5). Predict which of the two blue tangent lines is steeper, the x one or the y one. Check the readout. Then explain the ratio fy/fx = 4y/x using the two formulas.
