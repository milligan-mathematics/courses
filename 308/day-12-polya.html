How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 6.5.1

Why at least 35 elements

Exercise 6.5.1. Suppose that \(G\) is a finite group with an element \(g\) of order \(5\) and an element \(h\) of order \(7\). Why must \(|G| \geq 35\)?

Goal: Turn two facts about the orders of elements into one fact about the size of the group, and say which theorem makes each step work. It uses Lagrange’s Theorem (Theorem 6.2.2): the number of elements in a subgroup divides the number of elements in the group. It also uses the cyclic subgroup \(\langle g \rangle\) (Theorem 4.1.3).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 6.5.2

Which sizes can occur

Exercise 6.5.2. Suppose that \(G\) is a finite group with \(60\) elements. What are the orders of possible subgroups of \(G\)?

Goal: Use Lagrange’s Theorem to list every size a subgroup could have, and check that the candidates really occur in some group of \(60\) elements. It uses Lagrange’s Theorem (Theorem 6.2.2), and for the Look back the order of an element of a cyclic group (Theorem 4.1.13).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 6.5.5

Left and right cosets, part by part

Exercise 6.5.5. List the left and right cosets of the subgroups in each of the following.
(a) \(\langle 8 \rangle\) in \(\mathbb Z_{24}\)
(b) \(\langle 3 \rangle\) in \(U(8)\)
(c) \(3\mathbb Z\) in \(\mathbb Z\)
(d) \(A_4\) in \(S_4\)
(e) \(A_n\) in \(S_n\)
(f) \(D_4\) in \(S_4\)
(g) \(\mathbb T\) in \(\mathbb C^\ast\)
(h) \(H = \{(1), (1\,2\,3), (1\,3\,2)\}\) in \(S_4\)

Goal: Build left and right cosets by multiplying one fixed element through a subgroup, and see when the two lists agree and when they don’t. It uses the definitions of left and right cosets (Section 6.1), the fact that the left cosets partition the group (Theorem 6.1.4), and the count of left and right cosets (Theorem 6.1.8). The circle group \(\mathbb T\) is from Section 4.2.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 6.5.11

Five ways to say the same thing

Exercise 6.5.11. Let \(H\) be a subgroup of a group \(G\) and suppose that \(g_1, g_2 \in G\). Prove that the following conditions are equivalent.
(a) \(g_1 H = g_2 H\)
(b) \(H g_1^{-1} = H g_2^{-1}\)
(c) \(g_1 H \subset g_2 H\)
(d) \(g_2 \in g_1 H\)
(e) \(g_1^{-1} g_2 \in H\)

Goal: Find a route through all five conditions that closes up, and make each implication on it a short, checkable argument. It uses only the definition of cosets and the subgroup axioms. It is Lemma 6.1.3, which the book’s proof that the left cosets partition \(G\) (Theorem 6.1.4) relies on, and its (a) and (b) cases link left and right cosets.
