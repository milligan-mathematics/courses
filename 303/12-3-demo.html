The Moving TNB Frame

§12.3 — ride a point along a space curve and watch the T, N, B frame turn with it, with the osculating circle and live readouts of curvature, torsion, and acceleration.

Choose a curve and slide t. At the blue point you see three perpendicular unit vectors: T (the direction of travel), N (the direction the curve is bending), and B = T × N. The green circle is the osculating circle: the circle that best fits the curve at that point. Its radius is 1/κ, and its center lies along N. Pick one of the three planes to see it as a green sheet. Drag inside the scene to orbit it.

The frame arrows are drawn at a fixed display length (their true length is 1). The sheet is a patch of the full plane. The readouts always show the true values.

Things to try

  1. Pick the helix. Before you slide, predict which way N points (toward or away from the axis, and whether it tilts up or down). Then slide t and watch κ and τ. Do they change?
  2. Pick the twisted cubic. The section page found κ(t) and τ(t) for it. Predict κ and τ at t = 1 from those formulas, then type t = 1 and compare.
  3. Pick the ellipse, a curve that lies in a plane. Predict τ, and predict which way B points. Then predict where on the ellipse κ is largest, and check by sliding.
  4. Pick the spiral and slide t from 0 to 12. Predict whether the osculating circle grows or shrinks, and how aT and aN behave. Compare with the formulas in the worked example on the section page (at t = 1).
  5. On the cubic at t = 0, show the osculating plane. Predict how the curve sits relative to that sheet (does it stay on it?), then rotate the view to check. Do the same with the helix.
