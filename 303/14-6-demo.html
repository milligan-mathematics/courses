Warp the Grid

§14.6 — push a rectangular grid through a transformation, watch it bend, and compare the area of each image cell with the Jacobian times the area of the original cell.

The left picture is a rectangle in the uv-plane cut into m × n cells. The right picture is its image under the transformation x = g(u, v), y = h(u, v). Each cell lands on a bent four-sided piece of the xy-plane. The Jacobian predicts the area of that piece: |J| Δu Δv. The sum adds f(x, y) |J| Δu Δv over all cells, which is the change-of-variables formula as a Riemann sum, and the exact integral is shown for comparison. Use the two inspect sliders to pick a cell (shown in orange in both pictures).

The sample point is the middle of each cell in the uv-plane. The “true area” of an image cell is measured from its curved boundary, so for a small cell it is very close to the Jacobian prediction, and for a transformation like the linear one it is exactly equal.

Things to try

  1. Pick the linear map x = 3u − v, y = u + 2v with f = 1. Predict the area of every image cell compared with its original cell, then inspect two different cells to check. What is the area of the whole image?
  2. Pick x = u² − v², y = 2uv (from the section page). Predict which corner of the rectangle has the largest image cells, then inspect the cell in column 1, row 1 and the cell in the last column and last row. The Jacobian is 4(u² + v²); check the two readouts against it.
  3. Pick the polar map x = u cos v, y = u sin v with f = 1 and tick “Leave out the Jacobian”. Predict the wrong answer (it is just the area of the uv-rectangle), then untick it and compare with the exact area 3π/4.
  4. Pick the shear x = u + v², y = v. The grid lines bend into parabolas, so predict whether the image cells keep the area of the original cells. Then inspect a cell far from the origin.
  5. Pick the last map, the one from the double-integral example on the section page, with f = 1. Predict the area of the image of the rectangle [0, 2] × [0, 2] from the Jacobian 1/10, and check the exact area readout.
