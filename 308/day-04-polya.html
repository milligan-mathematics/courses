How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.9

Two matrices of determinant one

Exercise 3.5.9. Prove that the product of two matrices in \(SL_2(\mathbb R)\) has determinant one.

Goal: Show that matrices of determinant one stay that way under multiplication, by computing a determinant. It uses the matrix product of Example 3.2.7 and the set \(SL_2(\mathbb R)\) of Example 3.3.3, where Judson leaves this closure step to you. The general fact \(\det(AB) = \det A \det B\) is his Exercise 3.5.11; you will verify it for this case by multiplying out.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.14

Multiply the first parts, add the second

Exercise 3.5.14. Given the groups \(\mathbb R^*\) and \(\mathbb Z\), let \(G = \mathbb R^* \times \mathbb Z\). Define a binary operation \(\circ\) on \(G\) by \((a,m) \circ (b,n) = (ab, m + n)\). Show that \(G\) is a group under this operation.

Goal: Check the group axioms for a product of two groups, one coordinate at a time. It uses the coordinatewise operation on a product of groups, as in the \(\mathbb Z_2 \times \mathbb Z_2\) example of Example 3.3.5 (there with addition in both coordinates), and the same four axioms as Exercise 3.5.7 from Day 3.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.34

Counting subgroups

Exercise 3.5.34. Find all the subgroups of \(\mathbb Z_3 \times \mathbb Z_3\). Use this information to show that \(\mathbb Z_3 \times \mathbb Z_3\) is not the same group as \(\mathbb Z_9\). (See Example 3.3.5 for a short description of the product of groups.)

Goal: Find every subgroup of a small product of groups, then use the count to tell two groups apart, as Judson does in Example 3.3.5. It uses the definition of a subgroup from Section 3.3 and the coordinatewise operation of Example 3.3.5. The subgroup test (Proposition 3.3.7) comes later, so check each candidate from the definition.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.45

Where two subgroups overlap

Exercise 3.5.45. Prove that the intersection of two subgroups of a group \(G\) is also a subgroup of \(G\).

Goal: Prove a general fact about subgroups from the definition alone, then see why the same idea fails for unions. It uses the definition of a subgroup from Section 3.3: a subset that is a group under the operation of \(G\). The subgroup test (Proposition 3.3.7) comes later, so check each requirement by hand.
