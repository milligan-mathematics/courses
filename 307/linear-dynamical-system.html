Interactive model for studying bird population dynamics.

Matrix Recurrence Model

Consider the evolution of a species of bird. Let \( j_k \) be the number of juvenile females and \( a_k \) be the number of adult females in year \( k \). Based on our reproductive and survival assumptions:

  • \( j_{k+1} = 2a_k \) (The reproduction rate is 2)
  • \( a_{k+1} = 0.25j_k + 0.5a_k \) (25% of juveniles survive to adulthood, 50% of adults survive to the next year)

We can write this as a matrix recurrence \( \mathbf{v}_{k+1} = A\mathbf{v}_k \):

\[ \begin{bmatrix} j_{k+1} \\ a_{k+1} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 0.25 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} j_k \\ a_k \end{bmatrix} \]

Our initial population consists of 40 juvenile females and 100 adult females, so \( \mathbf{v}_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 40 \\ 100 \end{bmatrix} \).

Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

To understand the long-term behavior of the population, we find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of \( A \). Solving the characteristic equation \( \det(A - \lambda I) = 0 \):

\[ \det \begin{bmatrix} -\lambda & 2 \\ 0.25 & 0.5 - \lambda \end{bmatrix} = -\lambda(0.5 - \lambda) - (2)(0.25) = \lambda^2 - 0.5\lambda - 0.5 = 0 \]

Factoring gives \( (\lambda - 1)(\lambda + 0.5) = 0 \), so the eigenvalues are \( \lambda_1 = 1 \) and \( \lambda_2 = -0.5 \).

The corresponding eigenvectors are \( \mathbf{w}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \) and \( \mathbf{w}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} -4 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \).

Since the initial population is \( \mathbf{v}_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 40 \\ 100 \end{bmatrix} \), we can write it as a linear combination of the eigenvectors:

\[ \mathbf{v}_0 = c_1 \mathbf{w}_1 + c_2 \mathbf{w}_2 = \frac{220}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + \frac{80}{3} \begin{bmatrix} -4 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

For any year \( k \), the population is given by:

\[ \mathbf{v}_k = A^k \mathbf{v}_0 = c_1 (\lambda_1)^k \mathbf{w}_1 + c_2 (\lambda_2)^k \mathbf{w}_2 = \frac{220}{3} (1)^k \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + \frac{80}{3} (-0.5)^k \begin{bmatrix} -4 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Conclusion: As \( k \to \infty \), the term with \( (-0.5)^k \) approaches 0 because its magnitude is less than 1. The dominant eigenvalue is \( \lambda_1 = 1 \). This means that eventually, the population stabilizes and mimics the behavior of the eigenvector \( \mathbf{w}_1 \). The population will approach a ratio of 2 juveniles for every 1 adult, settling at approximately 146.7 juveniles and 73.3 adults.
