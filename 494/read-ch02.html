Chapter 2 · A

A space of ideas

A. Chapter 2, “Exploration.” Su sets Christopher, exploring math from a prison cell, beside his own childhood in rural Texas, exploring the stars through books and Voyager photos. Then he makes his claim: “Exploration is a deep human desire and a mark of human flourishing,” and you don’t need much except your mind to do it.

Goal: Follow Su’s case that mathematics is exploration, not memorization, then decide how far you agree. Exploration is the first of the desires in the book. Its virtues (imagination, creativity, an expectation of enchantment) come back in the chapters on play and beauty.

Chapter 2 · Puzzle

“Divides” Sudoku

Puzzle. A sudoku with no numbers at all. Fill the grid on page 32 so each row, column and 3-by-3 block holds 1 through 9 once. Whenever two neighbors in the same block have one value dividing the other, their common border is marked with \(\subset\), turned so that “A \(\subset\) B” means A divides B (the rounded side faces the divisor). A few “greater than” signs are also given. Su says it has a unique solution, and that the 1s are usually easy to place. (Su, Chapter 2.)

Goal: Get the puzzle started: place the 1s and fill most of one block, with a reason for every number. Divisibility among 1 through 9 is a small partial order. Reading off what it allows, and what it forbids, is the same habit you use with any relation in abstract algebra.
