Paper first, then check in. Give yourself 15 minutes for the five problems on paper, no calculator, the way the real test runs. Then open each card. If you already have an answer, press Check my answer from paper: one try. Right, and you can skip to Look back for a faster route or a variation. Not right, or not sure, and the walk-through takes it one step at a time: Understand, Plan, Carry out, Look back.

Every wrong choice has a story. The test writers build the wrong answers out of the slips people actually make. When you pick one here, the page tells you which slip leads there, so you can spot it next time.

Walk-through · Set B4, Problem 1

Which series converges?

1. Which of the following series converges?
(A) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\)
(B) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{n+1}\)
(C) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(-1\right)^n\frac{n}{n+1}\)
(D) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n\ln n}\)
(E) \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^2}{2^n}\)

Goal: Sort five series quickly: knock out what the divergence test can, then name the right test for each one left. “Which series converges?” is on nearly every MFT. Knowing the standard families (\(p\)-series, geometric, \(\frac{1}{n\ln n}\)) and which test fits which shape is most of the work.

Walk-through · Set B4, Problem 2

An integral you can’t do in the order given

2. \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\!\int_y^1 e^{x^2}\,dx\,dy=\)
(A) \(\frac{e-1}{2}\)
(B) \(e-1\)
(C) \(\frac{e}{2}\)
(D) \(\frac{e+1}{2}\)
(E) \(2(e-1)\)

Goal: Reverse the order of integration so that the inner integral becomes one you can do. When an inner integral has no elementary antiderivative (\(e^{x^2}\), \(\sin(x^2)\), \(\frac{\sin x}{x}\)), the MFT is almost always asking you to reverse the order. The region sketch is the whole problem.

Walk-through · Set B4, Problem 3

What every group of order 12 has

3. Let \(G\) be a group of order \(12\). Which of the following must be true?
  I. \(G\) has an element of order \(2\).
  II. \(G\) has an element of order \(6\).
  III. \(G\) has a subgroup of order \(6\).
(A) None
(B) I only
(C) I and II only
(D) I and III only
(E) I, II, and III

Goal: Separate what Cauchy’s and Lagrange’s theorems guarantee from what they don’t, and test the rest on \(A_4\). Lagrange (orders divide), Cauchy (primes dividing the order give elements), and the standard example \(A_4\) settle most MFT questions about groups of a given order.

Walk-through · Set B4, Problem 4

The rationals between 0 and 1

4. In \(\mathbb{R}\) with the usual topology, let \(A=\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)\), the set of rational numbers strictly between \(0\) and \(1\). Which of the following gives the interior and the closure of \(A\)?
(A) interior \((0,1)\), closure \([0,1]\)
(B) interior \(\varnothing\), closure \([0,1]\)
(C) interior \(\varnothing\), closure \(\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]\)
(D) interior \(A\), closure \((0,1)\)
(E) interior \(\varnothing\), closure \(\mathbb{R}\)

Goal: Use the definitions of interior and closure directly, testing a point at a time. The MFT’s topology questions are mostly about the real line: interior, closure, boundary, open and closed sets. The rationals and irrationals are the favorite examples, because both are dense.

Walk-through · Set B4, Problem 5

Coffee cooling in a room

5. A cup of coffee at 90°C is placed in a room kept at 20°C. After 10 minutes the coffee is at 60°C. Assuming Newton’s law of cooling, what is its temperature, in °C, after 20 minutes?
(A) \(30\)
(B) \(\frac{260}{7}\)
(C) \(40\)
(D) \(\frac{300}{7}\)
(E) \(\frac{340}{7}\)

Goal: Track the coffee’s excess over room temperature, which shrinks by the same factor every 10 minutes. Newton’s law of cooling is the MFT’s standard exponential-change model, alongside growth and decay and mixing problems. The key move is always to find the quantity that decays exponentially.
