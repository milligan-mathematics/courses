Chapter 9 · A

What kind of power?

A. Chapter 9, “Power.” Su starts with a deck of fifty-two cards and a gut-reaction question: which is largest, the number of stars in the universe, the number of seconds since the big bang, or the number of ways to order the deck? Then he says: “Power is a universal human desire. Yet power often sounds like a bad word.”

Goal: Follow Su’s untangling of good and bad power, and decide where you see each in mathematics. Chapter 10, “Justice,” picks up the coercive side: who gets shut out of mathematics, and what to do about it. The criterion at the end of this chapter is worth keeping for your Math & Faith paper.

Chapter 9 · Puzzle

Power Indices

Puzzle. A body of 100 votes is split into voting blocs: group A has 48, group B has 49, and group C has 3. A bill needs 51 votes, and each group votes together. Imagine the groups entering a room one at a time; the group whose arrival first brings the coalition to 51 is pivotal. A group’s Shapley–Shubik index is the fraction of the orderings in which it is pivotal. Find each group’s index. (Su, end of Chapter 9.)

Goal: Compute the three power indices, and explain why they come out the way they do. Su’s own example (50, 49, 1) gave the lone senator as much power as 49 others. This version is his 2017 Senate story with three senators as the small bloc.
