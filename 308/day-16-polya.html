How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.17

Two factors that can’t commute

Exercise 9.4.17. Prove that \(D_4\) cannot be the internal direct product of two of its proper subgroups.

Goal: Test the definition of an internal direct product on \(D_4\), then find the one fact about groups of order \(4\) that finishes the argument. It uses the definition of an internal direct product and Theorem 9.2.15, Lagrange’s Theorem (6.2.2), and the fact that cyclic groups are abelian. Example 9.2.14 treats \(S_3\) with a different argument.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.18

Powers of 2 and 3 make a copy of Z × Z

Exercise 9.4.18. Prove that the subgroup of \({\mathbb Q}^\ast\) consisting of elements of the form \(2^m 3^n\) for \(m,n \in {\mathbb Z}\) is an internal direct product isomorphic to \({\mathbb Z} \times {\mathbb Z}\).

Goal: Check the definition of an internal direct product on two infinite subgroups, then build the isomorphism from \({\mathbb Z} \times {\mathbb Z}\) and check that it is one-to-one. It uses the definition of an internal direct product (Section 9.2), Theorem 9.2.15, and the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (Theorem 2.2.7). Lagrange’s Theorem does not apply, since the group is infinite.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.19

Two ways to build the same hexagon

Exercise 9.4.19. Prove that \(S_3 \times {\mathbb Z}_2\) is isomorphic to \(D_6\). Can you make a conjecture about \(D_{2n}\)? Prove your conjecture.

Goal: Find an internal direct product inside \(D_6\), build the isomorphism with \(S_3\), and then see which conjecture about \(D_{2n}\) the same method supports. It uses Example 9.2.13 (\(D_6\) as an internal direct product), Theorem 9.2.15, and the multiplication of \(S_3\) from Chapter 3. \(D_n\) is the group of symmetries of a regular \(n\)-gon, of order \(2n\).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.33

One permutation for each element

Exercise 9.4.33. Write out the permutations associated with each element of \(S_3\) in the proof of Cayley’s Theorem.

Goal: Write out Cayley’s permutation for every element of \(S_3\), keeping track of which set each permutation acts on. It uses the proof of Cayley’s Theorem (Theorem 9.1.12) and the multiplication table of \(S_3\) from Chapter 3. The Day 16 page works the same idea for \({\mathbb Z}_4\).
