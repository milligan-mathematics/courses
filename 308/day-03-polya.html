How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.1

Congruences you can try out

Exercise 3.5.1. Find all \(x \in \mathbb Z\) satisfying each of the following equations.
(a) \(3x \equiv 2 \pmod{7}\)
(e) \(5x \equiv 1 \pmod{6}\)
(f) \(3x \equiv 1 \pmod{6}\)

Goal: Solve a linear congruence by trying the values that matter, and say when there are no solutions at all. It uses the integers mod \(n\) from Section 3.1, including Proposition 3.1.4 on inverses mod \(n\). It is the first equation in this course solved mod \(n\), and it sets up the group \(\mathbb Z_n\) in Exercise 3.5.2 and the Cayley tables in Exercise 3.5.3.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.2

Four tables, one test first

Exercise 3.5.2. Which of the following multiplication tables defined on the set \(G = \{ a, b, c, d \}\) form a group? Support your answer in each case.
(a) \[\begin{array}{c|cccc} \circ & a & b & c & d \\ \hline a & a & c & d & a \\ b & b & b & c & d \\ c & c & d & a & b \\ d & d & a & b & c \end{array}\](b) \[\begin{array}{c|cccc} \circ & a & b & c & d \\ \hline a & a & b & c & d \\ b & b & a & d & c \\ c & c & d & a & b \\ d & d & c & b & a \end{array}\](c) \[\begin{array}{c|cccc} \circ & a & b & c & d \\ \hline a & a & b & c & d \\ b & b & c & d & a \\ c & c & d & a & b \\ d & d & a & b & c \end{array}\](d) \[\begin{array}{c|cccc} \circ & a & b & c & d \\ \hline a & a & b & c & d \\ b & b & a & c & d \\ c & c & b & a & d \\ d & d & d & b & c \end{array}\]

Goal: Decide whether a table is a group by finding a quick reason to say no, and support a yes with all four axioms. It uses the four group axioms from Section 3.2, the identity and inverse facts in the reading, and the symmetries of a rectangle that Exercise 3.5.3 builds on.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.3

A rectangle and Z_4

Exercise 3.5.3. Write out Cayley tables for groups formed by the symmetries of a rectangle and for \((\mathbb Z_4, +)\). How many elements are in each group? Are the groups the same? Why or why not?

Goal: Build two Cayley tables from their definitions, and decide whether the groups are the same by a property that survives any renaming. It uses the symmetries of a rectangle from Section 3.1 and the integers mod \(n\). It leads to Exercise 3.5.2 (a table that matches the rectangle), and to the question of when two groups are the same, which Chapter 9 takes up.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.7

A strange product that is a group

Exercise 3.5.7. Let \(S = \mathbb R \setminus \{-1\}\) and define a binary operation on \(S\) by \(a \ast b = a + b + ab\). Prove that \((S, \ast)\) is an abelian group.

Goal: Check every group axiom for an operation you’ve never seen, and find the one idea that makes closure work. It uses the definition of a group from Section 3.2 and builds on the Cayley-table checks in Exercises 3.5.2 and 3.5.3; the same four axioms come back in every chapter after this one.
