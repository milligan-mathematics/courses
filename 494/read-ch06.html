Chapter 6 · A

What in math can you trust?

A. Chapter 6, “Permanence.” Su starts with an old flannel shirt he refuses to throw away: “We all long to have fixtures in our lives, things we can depend on.” Then he asks why we long for permanence, and what mathematics, where a proved theorem “will never be overturned,” has to offer that longing.

Goal: Follow Su’s three reasons we seek permanence and the question underneath them, then decide how far you agree. Chapter 5 ended with the chessboard puzzle; this chapter solves it with an invariant. Chapter 7 (truth) picks up the trust question and asks what it takes to know something deeply.

Chapter 6 · Puzzle

Shoelace Clock

Puzzle. You have a shoelace, matches and scissors. Lit at one end, the shoelace burns like a fuse and takes exactly 60 minutes. The burn rate can vary along the lace, but it is symmetric: the rate at distance \(x\) from the left end equals the rate at distance \(x\) from the right end. Lighting both ends at once measures 30 minutes (wait until the flames meet). What is the shortest time interval you can measure? (Su, end of Chapter 6. He also asks about two identical laces; that one is for our check-in.)

Goal: Measure an interval much shorter than 30 minutes, with a method that works whatever the burn rate is. This is the chapter’s theme in puzzle form: the burn rate is unknown and can be anything, so you have to build only on what can’t change.
