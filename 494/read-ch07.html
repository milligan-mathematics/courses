Chapter 7 · A

Why bother with truth?

A. Chapter 7, “Truth.” Su opens with a sixth-grade friend telling him he was adopted, a truth that “popped my bubble of denial.” Then he turns to a public that echoes Pilate’s “What is truth?” and asks: “Why should anyone bother?”

Goal: Follow Su’s case that math builds the habits of deep knowledge and deep investigation, then decide how far you agree. Chapter 6 said mathematical truth is permanent and asked whom we can trust. This chapter asks what it takes to know a truth deeply, and what is at stake if we don’t.

Chapter 7 · Puzzle

Vickrey Auction

Puzzle. You sell your car by silent auction. Each buyer seals one bid in an envelope; the highest bidder gets the car but pays the second-highest bid. This is a Vickrey auction. Why does it make telling the truth (bidding exactly what you think the car is worth) a better strategy than overbidding or underbidding? (Su, end of Chapter 7.)

Goal: Show that bidding your true value is never worse than any other bid, whatever the other buyers do. Su’s chapter is about loving the truth; this puzzle is mathematics designed so that the truth is in your own interest. He notes that Google Ads uses a generalization of it.
