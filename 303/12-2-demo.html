Inscribed Polygons and the Unit Tangent

§12.2 — cut a space curve into straight chords, watch their total length close in on the arc length, and watch T(t) ride along the curve.

The blue polygon connects n points on the curve with straight chords. Its total length Pn is always a little short of the true arc length L; more chords means a smaller gap. Slide t to move the blue point: the orange arrow is the unit tangent vector T(t), and the thick orange part of the curve is the distance s(t) traveled so far. Drag inside the scene to orbit it.

Arrows are drawn at a fixed fraction of their true length so they fit in the picture (the fraction is shown under the legend). The readouts always show the true values.

Things to try

  1. Pick the parabola with n = 1. Predict the single chord's length by hand (it joins the two end points), then check the readout. Raise n and watch the table: about how many chords does it take to get within 1% of L?
  2. Pick the helix. Before you switch “place the chord endpoints” between equal steps in t and equal steps in arc length, predict whether the picture will change. Then do the same on the parabola.
  3. Pick ⟨cos t, sin t, cos 3t⟩ and type t = 0.5236 (that is π/6). Predict the speed and the three components of T, then compare with the worked example on the section page.
  4. On the parabola, tick “also show r′(t)” and slide t from 0 to 2. Which of the two arrows changes length, and which one never does? What stays the same about their directions?
  5. Pick the ellipse (no closed form for its perimeter) and keep doubling n. Predict whether Pn can ever be larger than L, and check the table.
