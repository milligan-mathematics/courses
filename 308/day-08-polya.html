How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.20

The sixth roots of unity

Exercise 4.5.20. List and graph the 6th roots of unity. What are the generators of this group? What are the primitive 6th roots of unity?

Goal: Find all solutions of \(z^6 = 1\), then sort them out: which of them generate the whole group, and why. It uses DeMoivre’s theorem (Theorem 4.2.7), the roots of unity (Theorem 4.2.10), and the order rule from Day 7 (Theorem 4.1.13).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.21

The fifth roots of unity

Exercise 4.5.21. List and graph the 5th roots of unity. What are the generators of this group? What are the primitive 5th roots of unity?

Goal: Do Exercise 4.5.20 again for \(n = 5\), with less help. The new question is what changes when the number of roots is prime. It uses the same tools (DeMoivre’s theorem, the order rule from Theorem 4.1.13), and it leads into Exercise 4.5.26, which is about \(\mathbb Z_p\) for prime \(p\).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.26

Z_p has no nontrivial subgroups

Exercise 4.5.26. Prove that \({\mathbb Z}_{p}\) has no nontrivial subgroups if \(p\) is prime.

Goal: Show that a subgroup of \(\mathbb Z_p\) is either \(\{0\}\) or all of \(\mathbb Z_p\), using only what the chapter has reached so far. It uses the generators of \(\mathbb Z_n\) (Corollary 4.1.14). The order rule (Theorem 4.1.13) says the same thing in terms of orders: every nonzero element of \(\mathbb Z_p\) has order \(p\).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 4.5.46

Rational angles on the circle

Exercise 4.5.46. Let \(z =\cos \theta + i \sin \theta\) be in \({\mathbb T}\) where \(\theta \in {\mathbb Q}\). Prove that the order of \(z\) is infinite.

Goal: Show that no positive power of \(z\) equals \(1\), using the angle of \(z\) and the fact that \(\pi\) is irrational. Angles are in radians here: in degrees the claim would fail, since \(\operatorname{cis}90^\circ\) has order \(4\). It uses DeMoivre’s theorem (Theorem 4.2.7) and the definition of the order of an element. Check the statement at \(\theta = 0\) before you start the proof.
