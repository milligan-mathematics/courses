How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.1

The integers, scaled by n

Exercise 9.4.1. Prove that \(\mathbb Z \cong n \mathbb Z\) for \(n \neq 0\).

Goal: Find a map from \(\mathbb Z\) onto \(n\mathbb Z\) that carries addition along, then check that it is one-to-one and onto. It applies the definition of an isomorphism from Section 9.1. The look-back uses Theorem 9.1.7, which says every infinite cyclic group is isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z\), as a second route to the same conclusion.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.2

Complex numbers as matrices

Exercise 9.4.2. Prove that \(\mathbb C^\ast\) is isomorphic to the subgroup of \(GL_2(\mathbb R)\) consisting of matrices of the form \(\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ -b & a \end{pmatrix}\).

Goal: Find a map from \(\mathbb C^\ast\) to those matrices that carries multiplication to matrix multiplication, and check that it is one-to-one and onto. It uses the definition of an isomorphism (Section 9.1) and the group \(GL_2(\mathbb R)\) from Section 3.1, where the invertible matrices are the ones with nonzero determinant.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.8

Rationals are not the integers

Exercise 9.4.8. Prove that \(\mathbb Q\) is not isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z\).

Goal: Find a property that \(\mathbb Z\) has and \(\mathbb Q\) does not, and show that an isomorphism would have to keep it. It uses the definition of an isomorphism (Section 9.1) and the fact that an isomorphism carries a cyclic group to a cyclic group (Theorem 9.1.6(4)). The Carry out step proves that fact for this case.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 9.4.9

A strange product that is secretly multiplication

Exercise 9.4.9. Let \(G = \mathbb R \setminus \{-1\}\) and define a binary operation on \(G\) by \(a \ast b = a + b + ab\). Prove that \(G\) is a group under this operation. Show that \((G, \ast)\) is isomorphic to the multiplicative group of nonzero real numbers.

Goal: Find a relabeling of the elements that turns the strange operation into ordinary multiplication, then check that it is a bijection that carries one operation to the other. The group part is Exercise 3.5.7 (Day 3), the same set and the same operation. This exercise builds on what you found there, and explains why \(-1\) had to come out of \(\mathbb R\).
