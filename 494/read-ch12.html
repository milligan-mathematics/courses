Chapter 12 · A

Who belongs here?

A. Chapter 12, “Community.” Su opens with Ricardo Gutierrez, an audio engineer who went back to school at forty to study math and computer science and found the hardest part was the feeling “I’m not supposed to be here.” Community, Su argues, is a deep human desire, and the place where mathematical belonging is won or lost.

Goal: Follow Su’s case that mathematics is communal, name the virtues he asks for, and decide how far you agree. Chapter 13, “Love,” the last chapter, calls love the greatest human desire, the one that serves all the others, community included.

Chapter 12 · Puzzle

Five Points on a Sphere

Puzzle. Take any five points on a sphere. Show that some four of them lie on one hemisphere, counting the hemisphere’s boundary as part of it. (Su, Chapter 12.)

Goal: Find an argument that works for every choice of five points, not just the ones you can picture. Su calls this the book’s final puzzle and a model of a beautiful problem: easy to state, surprising, and open to several approaches. The engine is the pigeonhole principle.
