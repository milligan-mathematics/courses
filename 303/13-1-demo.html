Level Curve Explorer

§13.1 — slice a surface at height c and watch the cut appear on the 3D graph and as a level curve on its contour map.

Pick a surface, then slide the height c. The green plane \(z = c\) slices the 3D graph, and the slice is drawn in orange on the surface. That same slice is the level curve \(f(x,y) = c\), drawn in orange on the contour map beside it. The green lines on the map are other level curves, at evenly spaced heights. The readout names the shape of the cut.

Drag to rotate · darker patches are steeper
  • Green plane at height z = c
  • Orange cut where the plane meets the surface
  • Gray surface
  • Gray floor: undefined (restricted surface only)
Hover over the map, or Tab to it and use the arrow keys, to read f at a point

Hover over the map to read f(x, y) at a point.

  • Green: other level curves, evenly spaced heights
  • Orange: the level curve at height c
  • Gray: undefined region (restricted surface only)
  • Dark dot: the point you are reading

Things to try

  1. Predict the ellipse. Choose the bowl and check "Hide the answer." Before you touch the slider, predict the shape of the cut at c = 4 and which direction it is stretched. Then slide to c = 4 and uncheck the box to see if you were right. Compare the numbers with the bowl worked example on the section page.
  2. Empty on purpose. Still on the bowl, predict what the cut looks like at c = −1 before you slide below zero. Then slide down and watch where the plane sits compared with the bowl. Why does the readout say empty?
  3. Spacing and steepness. With the green contours showing, predict: on the bowl, are the contours packed tightly near the vertex or farther out? Rotate the 3D view and check where the gray surface is darkest. Then switch to the tilted plane. Its contours are evenly spaced. What does even spacing say about how steep the plane is?
  4. The edge of the domain. Choose the restricted surface. Predict the shape of the cut at c = 0 and where the gray undefined region sits. Then slide c from 0 up to 2 and predict whether the green contours bunch up or spread out as you move away from the gray edge. Check the contour map, and compare the domain with the first worked example on the section page.
