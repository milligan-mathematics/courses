How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

One step asks you to name a \(\delta\). In part (c) of Problem 8.2.14 you type \(\delta\) as a formula in eps and a. The page then plays the adversary from Definition 8.1.7: it picks a challenge \(\varepsilon\), tries points \(x\) just inside \(\delta\) of \(a\), and checks whether \(\cos x\) stays within \(\varepsilon\) of \(\cos a\). Any \(\delta\) that survives is right, not only the one in the model proof. Passing is evidence, not proof, and the proof is still yours to write.

Several of these problems end in a proof, so the proof you bring to class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.2.11

Dividing continuous functions

Problem 8.2.11. Use Theorem 8.2.1 to prove that if \(f\) and \(g\) are continuous at \(a\) and \(g(a) \neq 0\), then \(f/g\) is continuous at \(a\).

Goal: Turn continuity of \(f/g\) into a statement about sequences, then use the quotient theorem for sequences. Test \(f/g\) along sequences in the domains of \(f\) and \(g\), as the reading does, so \(g(x_n)\) may be \(0\) for a few early terms, where the quotient is simply undefined. The one thing to watch: \(g(a) \neq 0\) only guarantees nonzero values from some point on. It builds on Theorem 8.2.7 (sums and products of continuous functions) and on Lemma 8.2.9 from Day 25. You will use it in Problem 8.2.14 for rational functions and the trig quotients.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.2.13

Passing the baton through a composition

Problem 8.2.13. Prove Theorem 8.2.12: suppose \(f\) is continuous at \(a\) and \(g\) is continuous at \(f(a)\). Then \(g\circ f\) is continuous at \(a\), where \((g\circ f)(x) = g(f(x))\). Prove it (a) using the definition of continuity, and (b) using Theorem 8.2.1.

Goal: Prove the composition theorem two ways. In the ε–δ proof, \(g\)’s continuity at \(f(a)\) gives a number \(\eta\), and \(f\)’s continuity at \(a\), with \(\eta\) as its challenge, gives \(\delta\). It builds on the continuity facts of Days 22 to 25 and on Theorem 8.2.1. Problem 8.2.15 is a one-line use of it.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.2.14

Building the elementary functions

Problem 8.2.14. Show that each of the following is a continuous function at every point in its domain.
(a) Any polynomial.
(b) Any rational function (a ratio of polynomials).
(c) \(\cos x\).
(d) The other trig functions: \(\tan x\), \(\cot x\), \(\sec x\), and \(\csc x\).

Goal: Build the polynomials and rational functions from the base cases and the closure rules. Then do the one part that needs real work: \(\cos x\), from the definition, with a δ you can name. It uses Problems 8.1.9, 8.1.17 and 8.1.18, Theorem 8.2.7, and Problem 8.2.11 together. Part (c) is the one place in today’s set where the definition does the work.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.2.15

Composing two continuous functions

Problem 8.2.15. Explain why \(f(x) = \sin\left(e^x\right)\) is continuous. What allows us to conclude that \(f\) is continuous at any point \(a\) without referring back to the definition of continuity?

Goal: Name the inside and outside functions, check each one at the point the theorem needs, and let Theorem 8.2.12 do the rest. It is the first use of the composition theorem on a function from the reading’s examples. It previews Problem 8.2.16, where the same idea moves a limit inside a function.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.2.16

Limits through continuous functions

Problem 8.2.16. Compute the following limits. Be sure to point out how continuity is involved.
(a) \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi}{2n+1}\right)\)
(b) \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{\frac{n}{n^2+1}}\)
(c) \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{\sin\left(1/n\right)}\)

Goal: For each limit, find the limit of the inside sequence first, then move it through the outside function, which must be continuous at that limit. Theorem 8.2.1 is the bridge. It uses Problems 8.1.13, 8.1.17 and 8.1.18, and the limit laws and squeeze theorem of Chapter 6 for the inside limits. The last part ends in a proof-style write-up, and the look back checks part (a) with a cosine.
