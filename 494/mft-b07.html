Paper first, then check in. Give yourself 15 minutes for the five problems on paper, no calculator, the way the real test runs. Then open each card. If you already have an answer, press Check my answer from paper: one try. Right, and you can skip to Look back for a faster route or a variation. Not right, or not sure, and the walk-through takes it one step at a time: Understand, Plan, Carry out, Look back.

Every wrong choice has a story. The test writers build the wrong answers out of the slips people actually make. When you pick one here, the page tells you which slip leads there, so you can spot it next time.

Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back. Bring the one card that bugged you most to our check-in.

Walk-through · Set B7, Problem 1

A limit of the form one to the infinity

1. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{3}{x}\right)^{2x}=\)
(A) \(1\)
(B) \(e^{3/2}\)
(C) \(e^{2}\)
(D) \(e^{3}\)
(E) \(e^{6}\)

Goal: Take logarithms to turn the power into a product you can handle. Limits like \(\left(1 + \frac ax\right)^{bx}\) are the MFT’s favorite \(1^\infty\) form. They also sit behind continuous compound interest, \(Pe^{rt}\).

Walk-through · Set B7, Problem 2

Rotate, then reflect

2. Let \(T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2\) rotate each vector \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise about the origin and then reflect it across the \(x\)-axis. What is the standard matrix of \(T\)?
(A) \(\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}\)
(B) \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\)
(C) \(\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\)
(D) \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}\)
(E) \(\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}\)

Goal: Find where \(T\) sends \(\mathbf e_1\) and \(\mathbf e_2\); those images are the columns. “The columns are the images of the basis vectors” answers most MFT questions about matrices of transformations, and composition order (first map on the right) is the usual trap.

Walk-through · Set B7, Problem 3

Complex characteristic roots

3. Which of the following is the general solution of \(y’’-4y’+13y=0\)?
(A) \(y=e^{3x}(c_1\cos 2x+c_2\sin 2x)\)
(B) \(y=e^{2x}(c_1\cos 3x+c_2\sin 3x)\)
(C) \(y=e^{-2x}(c_1\cos 3x+c_2\sin 3x)\)
(D) \(y=c_1e^{4x}+c_2e^{13x}\)
(E) \(y=c_1\cos(\sqrt{13}\,x)+c_2\sin(\sqrt{13}\,x)\)

Goal: Solve the characteristic equation and read the solution off its complex roots. Constant-coefficient linear ODEs are the most common differential equations item on the MFT, and in engineering they’re springs and circuits.

Walk-through · Set B7, Problem 4

The biggest angle of a triangle

4. A triangle has sides of length \(7\), \(8\), and \(13\). What is the measure of its largest angle?
(A) \(30^\circ\)
(B) \(60^\circ\)
(C) \(90^\circ\)
(D) \(120^\circ\)
(E) \(150^\circ\)

Goal: Use the law of cosines on the angle opposite the longest side. The law of cosines is the MFT’s main triangle tool, and comparing \(c^2\) with \(a^2 + b^2\) (acute, right or obtuse) is a fast way to cut choices.

Walk-through · Set B7, Problem 5

Customers switching phone companies

5. Each month, \(80\%\) of the customers of phone company A stay with A and \(20\%\) switch to company B. Of B’s customers, \(70\%\) stay with B and \(30\%\) switch to A. If no customers enter or leave the market, what fraction of the customers will A have in the long run?
(A) \(0.4\)
(B) \(0.5\)
(C) \(0.6\)
(D) \(0.7\)
(E) \(0.8\)

Goal: Find the split where the customers flowing each way balance, so the shares stop changing. Steady states of Markov chains are the MFT’s favorite applied linear algebra: the long-run vector is the eigenvector for eigenvalue \(1\). Balancing the flows gets it fastest for two states.
