Trace the Curve

§12.1 — slide the parameter t, watch a point trace a space curve, and read off the position vector, the velocity vector r′(t), and the speed.

Choose a curve, then slide t (or type a value, or press Play). The blue point is the tip of the position vector r(t); the blue part of the curve is what has been traced so far. The orange arrow is the velocity vector r′(t): it is tangent to the curve and its length is the speed. Drag inside the scene to orbit it.

The arrow is drawn at a fixed fraction of its true length so that it fits in the picture (the fraction is shown under the legend). The readouts always show the true values.

Things to try

  1. Pick the helix. Before you turn on the shadow, predict what shape its shadow in the xy-plane will be. Then tick the box.
  2. On the helix, type 0.7854 (that is π/4) for t. Predict the three components of r′(t) first, then compare with the readout. This is the point in the tangent-line example on the section page.
  3. Pick the tilted ellipse. Predict the height z of the point when t = 3.14, and where on the curve it is lowest. Then slide to check.
  4. Pick the trefoil and press Play. Is the speed constant? Make a prediction, then watch the speed readout, and compare with the helix.
  5. Pick the cusp and slide t slowly from −1 up through 0. Predict what the velocity arrow does as it passes 0, then check. Is the curve smooth there?
