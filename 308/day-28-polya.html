How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 16.7.1

Eight sets: rings, and which are fields

Exercise 16.7.1. Which of the following sets are rings with respect to the usual operations of addition and multiplication? If the set is a ring, is it also a field?
(a) \(7\mathbb Z\)
(b) \(\mathbb Z_{18}\)
(c) \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2) = \{a + b\sqrt2 : a, b \in \mathbb Q\}\)
(d) \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2, \sqrt3) = \{a + b\sqrt2 + c\sqrt3 + d\sqrt6 : a, b, c, d \in \mathbb Q\}\)
(e) \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt3] = \{a + b\sqrt3 : a, b \in \mathbb Z\}\)
(f) \(R = \{a + b\sqrt[3]{3} : a, b \in \mathbb Q\}\)
(g) \(\mathbb Z[i] = \{a + bi : a, b \in \mathbb Z \text{ and } i^2 = -1\}\)
(h) \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{3}) = \{a + b\sqrt[3]{3} + c\sqrt[3]{9} : a, b, c \in \mathbb Q\}\)

Goal: Decide, for eight sets of numbers, whether each is a ring and whether it is a field, and be able to say why in a sentence or two. It uses the definitions of ring, integral domain and field from Sections 16.1 and 16.2, the subring test (Proposition 16.1.10), and the idea behind Theorem 16.2.5 (a finite integral domain is a field). Example 16.2.3 shows that \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) is a field.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 16.7.3

Units in five rings

Exercise 16.7.3. List or characterize all of the units in each of the following rings.
(a) \(\mathbb Z_{10}\)
(b) \(\mathbb Z_{12}\)
(c) \(\mathbb Z_7\)
(d) \(\mathbb M_2(\mathbb Z)\), the \(2 \times 2\) matrices with entries in \(\mathbb Z\)
(e) \(\mathbb M_2(\mathbb Z_2)\), the \(2 \times 2\) matrices with entries in \(\mathbb Z_2\)

Goal: Say which elements of each ring are units, and explain why a unit’s inverse has to lie inside the ring itself. It uses the definition of a unit from Section 16.2 (Example 16.2.1 finds the units of \(\mathbb Z[i]\)), the units mod \(n\) from Chapter 3, and the matrix groups of Chapter 12: the units of a matrix ring are a group of matrices.
