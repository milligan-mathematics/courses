Riding a Path Over a Surface

§13.4 — lift a path in the plane onto a surface, ride a point along it, and watch the two chain-rule terms add up to dz/dt.

Choose a path and a surface, then slide the point along the path or press Play. The point's height is z = f(x, y). Each route in the tree contributes one term to dz/dt: the partial derivative times the velocity along that route. The bars show the two terms and their sum. Drag inside the scene to orbit it.

The height is drawn to fit the view, so the picture is not to scale. The readouts, bars, graph, and tree use the true values.

Things to try

  1. Predict, then check (preset 2). Set t = 2.5. Before you reveal the graph, use the two bars to predict whether z is rising or falling at that moment. Then press Show the z(t) graph and check the slope of the tangent line.
  2. A zero with no zero terms (preset 3). Find a t where dz/dt = 0 even though neither bar is zero. Predict first: where must the two bars be equal and opposite? Then check the readouts.
  3. Connect to the cylinder (preset 3). In the cylinder example on the section page, the two terms cancel at the largest volume, at t = 4. Predict whether z also peaks where the bars cancel in this preset, then reveal the graph and find out.
  4. Check the tree (any preset). Pick one route and multiply its two labels, such as the route through x. Predict that the product matches that route's bar, then check it against the bar chart. Add the two routes to confirm that the sum is dz/dt.
