How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

Something new: you type formulas. Some steps ask for a formula in the letters named in the question. Use ^ for powers, write sin( ), cos( ) and exp( ), use pi for \(\pi\), and write Greek letters as words, so \(\alpha\) is alpha. The page checks your formula at several test values, so any formula that gives the right values is right, not only the one in the model.

The walk-throughs end in full proofs, so the proof you bring to class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 4.1.7

The derivatives step down, one factor at a time

Problem 4.1.7. Use Taylor’s formula to obtain the general binomial series \(\displaystyle (1+x)^\alpha=1+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\alpha-j\right)}{n!}x^n.\)

Goal: Find the coefficients of \((1+x)^\alpha\) for every \(\alpha\): find the pattern in the derivatives, prove it by induction, and divide by \(n!\). It builds on Day 9’s binomial series, which was right for every \(\alpha\) but not yet proved, and on the geometric series for \(\alpha = -1\). It leads to 4.1.8, where the same method gives the series of \(e^x\), \(\sin x\), and \(\cos x\).

Pólya walk-through · Problem 4.1.8

Derivatives about \(a\), and the cycle that repeats

Problem 4.1.8. Use Taylor’s formula to obtain the Taylor series for the functions \(e^x\), \(\sin x\), and \(\cos x\) expanded about \(a\).

Goal: Compute the derivatives at \(a\), find the pattern for each function, and write the series with \(\frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(x-a)^n\). Then ask whether the remainder dies. It builds on 4.1.7, where the same method gave the coefficients at \(0\). Here the centre is any \(a\), and the question of whether the series equals the function becomes the central one. That is the remainder question of Theorem 4.1.9.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 4.1.10

One integration by parts at a time

Problem 4.1.10. Provide a formal induction proof for Theorem 4.1.9 (Taylor’s Theorem, integral form of the remainder). If \(f^{\prime}, f^{\prime\prime}, \ldots, f^{(n+1)}\) are all continuous on an interval containing \(a\) and \(x\), then \[f(x)=f(a)+\frac{f^{\prime}(a)}{1!}(x-a)+\cdots+\frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(x-a)^n+\frac{1}{n!}\int_a^x f^{(n+1)}(t)(x-t)^n\,dt.\]

Goal: Prove Theorem 4.1.9 by induction on \(n\). The base case is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. The inductive step is one integration by parts, and its boundary term at \(t = a\) is the next term of the Taylor polynomial. It builds on the reading’s derivation of the \(n = 1\) case and on the scaffold on the Day 13 page. It is the tool behind the remainder bound in 4.1.8, and the identity \(f = P_n + R_n\) that makes Day 12’s counterexample work.
