The Power Method

Estimating the dominant eigenvalue and eigenvector of a matrix.

Algorithm overview

The power method is a way to estimate the dominant eigenvalue/eigenvector pair of a matrix. Consider the fixed matrix:

\[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 5 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \]

Steps:

  1. Select an initial "seed" vector \(\mathbf{x}_0\) whose largest entry in absolute value is 1.
  2. For each step \( k = 0, 1, 2, \dots \):
    • Compute \( A\mathbf{x}_k \).
    • Let \( \mu_k \) be an entry in \( A\mathbf{x}_k \) whose absolute value is as large as possible.
    • Compute the next scaled vector: \( \mathbf{x}_{k+1} = (1/\mu_k) A\mathbf{x}_k \).

Why does this work?

Let us examine the true eigenvalues and eigenvectors of \( A = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 5 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \). Solving \( \det(A - \lambda I) = 0 \):

\[ (6-\lambda)(2-\lambda) - 5 = \lambda^2 - 8\lambda + 7 = (\lambda - 7)(\lambda - 1) = 0 \]

The eigenvalues are \( \lambda_1 = 7 \) and \( \lambda_2 = 1 \). The dominant eigenvalue is 7 (since \( |7| > |1| \)).

Finding the eigenvector for \( \lambda_1 = 7 \): \( A - 7I = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 5 \\ 1 & -5 \end{bmatrix} \), which gives the basis eigenvector \( \mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \).

If we scale \( \mathbf{v}_1 \) so that its largest entry is 1, we divide by 5: \( \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0.2 \end{bmatrix} \).

Conclusion: For almost all choices of \( \mathbf{x}_0 \), the sequence \( \{ \mu_k \} \) approaches the dominant eigenvalue (7), and the sequence \( \{ \mathbf{x}_k \} \) approaches the scaled dominant eigenvector \( \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0.2 \end{bmatrix} \).
