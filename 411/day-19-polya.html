How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

Something new: no formula for \(N\). Today the limits are given to you, and each one comes with its own threshold: \(N_1\) for one sequence, \(N_2\) for the other. The sum or product needs a single \(N\) that is past both. So the steps ask you to split the \(\varepsilon\) between the pieces, pick the right threshold, and say why each piece is needed.

The walk-throughs end in a full proof, so the proof you bring to class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 6.2.1

The distance that is always zero

Problem 6.2.1. Let \((c)_{n=1}^\infty = (c, c, c, \ldots)\) be a constant sequence. Show that \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} c = c\).

Goal: Use the definition of a limit (Definition 6.1.11) to show that a constant sequence converges to its own value. It is the smallest proof of the day, so the point is the definition, not the algebra. It is the fact behind Corollary 6.2.10 on Day 20: the product theorem, applied to a constant sequence, gives constant multiples.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 6.2.3

The Triangle Inequality from two facts about \(|x|\)

Problem 6.2.3. Prove the Triangle Inequality and its reverse form (Lemma 6.2.2). For real numbers \(a\) and \(b\), \(|a + b| \le |a| + |b|\), and \(|a| - |b| \le |a - b|\).

Goal: Prove two inequalities for all real \(a\) and \(b\). The first comes from two facts about \(|x|\) that hold for every \(x\). The second is the first one applied to the split \(a = (a - b) + b\). The reading also asks for the two-sided form \(\big||a| - |b|\big| \le |a - b|\); the Look back step gets it. The sum and product theorems (Problems 6.2.5 and 6.2.9) both split an error into pieces with the Triangle Inequality, so this is the tool they use.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 6.2.5

One budget of \(\varepsilon\), two errors

Problem 6.2.5. Prove the sum theorem (Theorem 6.2.4). If \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = a\) and \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} b_n = b\), then \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(a_n + b_n) = a + b\).

Goal: Given two limits, find one \(N\) past which \(|(a_n + b_n) - (a + b)| < \varepsilon\). The Triangle Inequality splits the error in two. The work is in dividing \(\varepsilon\) between the pieces and taking the larger of the two thresholds. The product theorem (Problem 6.2.9) uses the same plan, with one more idea: the bound from Lemma 6.2.7.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 6.2.8

A convergent sequence is bounded

Problem 6.2.8. Prove that a convergent sequence is bounded (Lemma 6.2.7). If \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = a\), then there is \(B > 0\) such that \(|a_n| \le B\) for every \(n\).

Goal: Show that a convergent sequence has one fixed bound \(B\) for all of its terms. The definition controls only the terms past some \(N\), so the early terms need their own argument. This \(B\) is what the product theorem (Problem 6.2.9) needs: it freezes \(|a_n|\) at one constant, so one threshold can serve every \(n\).

Pólya walk-through · Problem 6.2.9

Uncancel, freeze, and split

Problem 6.2.9. Prove the product theorem (Theorem 6.2.6). If \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = a\) and \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} b_n = b\), then \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n b_n = ab\).

Goal: Show that \(a_n b_n\) is within \(\varepsilon\) of \(ab\) past some \(N\). Separate the two sequences by uncancelling, freeze the moving factor \(|a_n|\) with a bound, and divide the \(\varepsilon\) between the two pieces. Day 20 faces the same dilemma in the quotient rule (Theorem 6.2.12): a moving factor, \(\frac{1}{|b_n|}\), needs a fixed bound.
