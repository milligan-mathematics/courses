Paper first, then check in. Give yourself 15 minutes for the five problems on paper, no calculator, the way the real test runs. Then open each card. If you already have an answer, press Check my answer from paper: one try. Right, and you can skip to Look back for a faster route or a variation. Not right, or not sure, and the walk-through takes it one step at a time: Understand, Plan, Carry out, Look back.

Every wrong choice has a story. The test writers build the wrong answers out of the slips people actually make. When you pick one here, the page tells you which slip leads there, so you can spot it next time.

Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back. Bring the one card that bugged you most to our check-in.

Walk-through · Set B5, Problem 1

A parabola and a line

1. What is the area of the region enclosed by the graphs of \(y=x^2\) and \(y=2x\)?
(A) \(\frac{4}{3}\)
(B) \(2\)
(C) \(\frac{8}{3}\)
(D) \(4\)
(E) \(\frac{16}{3}\)

Goal: Find where the curves meet, then integrate the top curve minus the bottom one. Area between curves is a standard MFT calculus item. The work is in the setup: the limits and which curve is on top.

Walk-through · Set B5, Problem 2

A determinant from eigenvalues

2. A \(3\times 3\) matrix \(A\) has eigenvalues \(1\), \(2\), and \(4\). What is \(\det(A^2+A)\)?
(A) \(8\)
(B) \(56\)
(C) \(72\)
(D) \(120\)
(E) \(240\)

Goal: Find the eigenvalues of \(A^2 + A\) from those of \(A\), then multiply them. “A polynomial in \(A\) has eigenvalues \(p(\lambda)\)” and “the determinant is the product of the eigenvalues” together answer a whole family of MFT linear algebra questions without ever seeing the matrix.

Walk-through · Set B5, Problem 3

Uniform continuity near a bad endpoint

3. Which of the following functions are uniformly continuous on the open interval \((0,1)\)?
  I. \(f(x)=x\sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\)
  II. \(g(x)=\frac{1}{x}\)
  III. \(h(x)=\sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\)
(A) None
(B) I only
(C) II only
(D) I and III only
(E) I, II, and III

Goal: Decide each function by asking whether it settles down to a limit as \(x \to 0^+\). Uniform continuity on a bounded open interval comes up in MFT analysis items and in real analysis courses. The extension test turns each case into a one-sided limit.

Walk-through · Set B5, Problem 4

Counting faces without a picture

4. A connected planar graph has \(8\) vertices and \(12\) edges. When it is drawn in the plane with no edges crossing, how many faces does it have, counting the unbounded face?
(A) \(2\)
(B) \(4\)
(C) \(5\)
(D) \(6\)
(E) \(20\)

Goal: Use Euler’s formula to get the number of faces from the vertices and edges alone. Euler’s formula \(V - E + F = 2\) is the one planar-graph fact the MFT asks about most, often combined with “every face has at least 3 edges” to show a graph can’t be planar.

Walk-through · Set B5, Problem 5

Customers at the bank

5. The number of customers who arrive at a bank in one hour follows a Poisson distribution with mean \(3\). What is the probability that exactly \(2\) customers arrive in a given hour?
(A) \(e^{-3}\)
(B) \(3e^{-3}\)
(C) \(\frac{9}{2}e^{-3}\)
(D) \(9e^{-3}\)
(E) \(1-4e^{-3}\)

Goal: Use the Poisson formula \(P(X = k) = \frac{\lambda^k e^{-\lambda}}{k!}\) with the right \(\lambda\) and \(k\). The Poisson distribution models counts of independent events in a fixed time or space (arrivals, defects, calls). On the MFT you’re expected to know its formula, mean and variance cold.
