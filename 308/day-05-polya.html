How these work. Each walk-through takes one exercise through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

The walk-throughs end in full solutions, so the work you present in class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.35

All the subgroups of a triangle’s symmetries

Exercise 3.5.35. Find all the subgroups of the symmetry group of an equilateral triangle.

Goal: Make a complete list of subgroups without guessing: let closure show what each set must contain, and test every candidate with the one-step test. It uses Judson’s table for the symmetries of a triangle (Figure 3.1.7) and the one-step test (Proposition 3.3.8). It is a list, so the work is careful checking rather than a proof.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.37

The powers of two form a subgroup

Exercise 3.5.37. Let \(H = \{2^k : k \in \mathbb Z\}\). Show that \(H\) is a subgroup of \(\mathbb Q^*\).

Goal: Show a set is a subgroup with the one-step test: pick two elements, form \(gh^{-1}\), and see that it stays in the set. It uses the one-step test (Proposition 3.3.8), the laws of exponents in a group (Theorem 3.2.16), and the fact that \(\mathbb Q^*\) is a subgroup of \(\mathbb R^*\) (Example 3.3.1).

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.39

The unit circle is a subgroup

Exercise 3.5.39. Let \(\mathbb T = \{ z \in \mathbb C^* : |z| = 1 \}\). Prove that \(\mathbb T\) is a subgroup of \(\mathbb C^*\).

Goal: Use the one-step test on the unit circle, and find the one fact about moduli that does all the work. It uses the one-step test (Proposition 3.3.8) and the group \(\mathbb C^*\) under multiplication (Example 3.2.9). The fact that \(|zw| = |z|\,|w|\) is a property of complex numbers, not of groups.

Pólya walk-through · Exercise 3.5.48

The center of a group

Exercise 3.5.48. Let \(G\) be a group and \(g \in G\). Show that \(Z(G) = \{x \in G : gx = xg \text{ for all } g \in G\}\) is a subgroup of \(G\). This subgroup is called the center of \(G\).

Goal: Show that the elements that commute with everything form a subgroup, using the three subgroup conditions, each checked from the definition. It uses the definition of a subgroup and the three-part test (Proposition 3.3.7). The center of \(D_3\) is one of the cases you can check by hand.
