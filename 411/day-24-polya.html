How these work. Each walk-through takes one problem through Pólya’s four phases. You answer one step at a time and get feedback right away; when something isn’t right, read why and try again. Hints are there when you ask for them. Your place is saved in this browser, so you can stop and come back.

Naming \(\delta\), as on Days 22 and 23. Some steps ask you to type \(\delta\) as a formula in \(\varepsilon\) (type eps), and in Problem 8.1.18(b) also in \(a\). The page plays the opponent: it names challenges from \(\varepsilon = 10\) down to \(\varepsilon = 0.000001\), and for each one it hunts for an \(x\) within your \(\delta\) of \(a\) whose value \(f(x)\) lands outside the band around \(f(a)\). Any \(\delta\) that works is right, not only the one in the model proof. The page samples many points, so passing is strong evidence, not a proof.

Problem 8.1.19 has a formula that can’t be evaluated at \(0\), so the page compares with the value \(f(0) = 0\) that the problem gives. Problem 8.1.20 has no formula to test, so its steps ask for choices, for lines of a proof, and finally for a proof.

The walk-throughs end in full proofs, so the proof you bring to class should still be your own, in your own words.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.1.18

Trap \(e^x\) with logarithms

Problem 8.1.18. Prove that \(e^x\) is continuous at \(0\) and at any point \(a\).
(a) Use the definition of continuity to show that \(f(x) = e^x\) is continuous at \(a = 0\).
(b) Show that \(f(x) = e^x\) is continuous at any point \(a\). Rewrite \(e^x - e^a\) as \(e^{a+(x-a)} - e^a\) and use what you proved in part (a).

Goal: Turn the band around \(e^x\) into a strip around \(0\) with logarithms, then move the result from \(a = 0\) to any \(a\). The proof uses that \(e^x\) is increasing. From Day 7: the series gives \(e^h > 1\) for \(h > 0\) and \(e^x > 0\) for \(x \ge 0\); Property 4 gives \(e^x > 0\) for \(x < 0\); and Property 2 gives \(e^{x+h} = e^x e^h\). The logarithm \(\ln\) is the inverse of \(e^x\) from calculus, so \(e^{\ln y} = y\) for \(y > 0\). Part (b) reduces to part (a) by one algebraic step, the way Day 18 reduced one limit to another.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.1.19

The envelope does the work

Problem 8.1.19. The topologist’s sine function. Use the definition of continuity to show that \(f(x) = x\sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\) for \(x \ne 0\), with \(f(0) = 0\), is continuous at \(0\).

Goal: Find a \(\delta\) for every \(\varepsilon\), even though the function oscillates faster and faster as \(x\) nears \(0\), and handle the point \(x = 0\) on its own. The envelope \(\pm|x|\) on the day page is the whole proof. Problem 8.1.20 has the same envelope at \(0\), with a function that behaves very differently everywhere else.

Pólya walk-through · Problem 8.1.20

Full of holes

Problem 8.1.20. Analyze Dirichlet’s function. (a) Use the definition of continuity to show that \(D(x) = x\) if \(x\) is rational and \(D(x) = 0\) if \(x\) is irrational, is continuous at \(0\).
(b) Let \(a \ne 0\). Use the definition of continuity to show that \(D\) is not continuous at \(a\). You might want to break this up into two cases, where \(a\) is rational or irrational.

Goal: Prove continuity at \(0\) with the envelope \(|D(x)| \le |x|\), then prove discontinuity at each \(a \ne 0\) by finding one \(\varepsilon\) that no \(\delta\) can beat. Problem 8.1.19 had the same envelope at \(0\), now with a function that is \(x\) on the rationals and \(0\) on the irrationals. Part (b) uses the density of the rationals and of the irrationals, Theorem 2.0.11 from Day 4.
